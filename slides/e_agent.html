Experiment 8  ·  Nine techniques vs AgentDojo

Half the agent techniques hold, and the main test gave us nothing

What its paper claims

Ingestion- and execution-stage techniques — filters, an LLM judge, paraphrasing, known-answer detection, tool-call goal consistency — detect or neutralise prompt injection in an agent.

What we ran

The AgentDojo banking suite, Qwen2.5-14B, 56 conditions, n = 32 user×injection task pairs each, every condition run defended and undefended.

What we got

24 of 56 hold with attack success driven to zero. 4 show a resolvable reduction, 19 no resolvable effect, 9 no reduction at all. Ingestion-stage techniques account for most of the holds.

Attack success rate here, so lower is better — the only place in this deck where the metric is ASR rather than accuracy.

Two things to say plainly. First, the metric changes here. In the retrieval cell we measure accuracy on the gold answer and higher is better. In the agent cell the unit is a complete multi-step trajectory and the question is binary: did the injection get the agent to do the attacker's task. Lower is better. Second, the primary experiment produced no data and we report that first. It targeted three mechanisms with zero recorded defenses — the actual transfer question — and the controls came back too weak to read: spoofing at 6.2% undefended, refusal induction inert at 0.0%. Ten cells are inconclusive, not successes. A defense that 'stops' an attack scoring 0% undefended has stopped nothing.
