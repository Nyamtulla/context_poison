Say the point before the grid: we did not pick case studies by familiarity. The taxonomy picks them. A pair is a candidate when the defense and the attack occupy the same cell — same channel in, same consequence out — and no paper in the corpus reports having run them together. The two dark squares are where the campaign lives, and they were chosen for opposite reasons. The figure on this slide is Figure 4 of the paper, the rendered file itself, so the two cannot drift apart. RAG x silent-corruption is dense and contested: 101 papers, 73 safety and 28 security. That split IS the argument — two literatures working one square without citing each other. That is the retrieval cell, all 34 pairs. tool-output x goal-hijack is the field's largest single attack surface at 172 papers, 170 of them security. We went there because it is where a security defense could be tested against something it was not built for. That is the agent cell, 56 pairs. This grid and Figure 4 in the paper are the same figure: same counts, same encoding, dark ring for attacks with no defense. The panels are REGIMES, not venues, and the distinction matters if anyone asks. Regime is what the work is about: is there an adversary, or does the context go wrong on its own. Venue is where it published, and the two are not the same cut — 53 of the 101 security-regime papers with a named venue published at an AI venue. The separation is in what these papers cite, not in where they appear. The empty-cell rate is 54.8%, computed over named attacks and defenses rather than over papers. A survey that ranges across six channels now occupies none of them, which is why the figure and the number finally agree. The pink cells are the sharpest thing on this slide: persistence-backdoor has named attacks almost everywhere and essentially no defenses, and supply-chain exfiltration has an attack and nothing against it. That is the coverage gap in one glance. If asked why only two cells: each one costs weeks of reconstruction work from released artifacts, and the defenses inside a single cell cannot even share a Python environment. Breadth here would mean citing other people's numbers, which is the thing we refuse to do.
