Seven of the ten are one family: the intervention is a weighting between the context-conditioned and the memory-conditioned prediction. Blue = built in the safety regime, for knowledge conflict; red = built in the security regime, for poisoned retrieval.

Eight of the nine retrieval defense implementations were built in the safety regime and run against security attacks — the crossing the literature never makes.

The family definition is mechanical, not a vibe: a method whose intervention is a weighting between the context-conditioned and the memory-conditioned prediction. That is what lets us say the result is about a design principle rather than a list of papers. DyPRAG and the two RobustRAG variants are deliberately outside it, and they are what keeps the claim falsifiable. DyPRAG has no context-versus-memory dial at all. RobustRAG asks WHICH passages agree rather than how far to trust passages in general. Models differ because each paper released what it released. That is why we ran a model control later. If asked what regime means here: it is what the work assumes, not where it published. A defense built for knowledge conflict is safety-regime even if it appeared at a security venue. The two are coded independently and they are not the same cut. FaithfulRAG and SHIFT appear here but produced no transfer verdict; slide 20 says why.
