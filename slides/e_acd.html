Experiment 4  ·  ACD vs corpus poisoning

ACD resists one payload and fails against the other

What its paper claims

Contrastive decoding weighted by how much uncertainty the context induces: discount passages that make the model less certain, trust the ones that make it more certain.

What we ran

Its released decoder in its own pinned environment, n = 400. Run against corpus poisoning, then against the benchmark’s own fluent knowledge-conflict passage, same items and doses.

What we got

Against the optimised, unreadable payload it is +9.2 and restores the no-retrieval floor exactly. Against the fluent payload the protection is gone.

n = 400, threshold 9.9pp. Same defense, same items, same doses — only the form of the planted passage differs.

The reason is visible in the method. ACD keys on the uncertainty a passage induces. An optimised token string is incoherent and raises uncertainty, so ACD discounts it. A fluent sentence asserting a plausible wrong answer LOWERS uncertainty, so ACD trusts it more. Say the limit before the result, not after: the corpus-poisoning passage was optimised against a retriever, not against ACD. A defense evaluated only against attacks not built for it has been shown to resist those attacks and nothing more. Practical consequence worth stating: the cheaper, more natural-looking attack is the stronger one. The field's attention to gradient-optimised passages is not matched by their effectiveness.
