Experiments that produced no verdict

Two defenses failed their own control, so we report no verdict

FaithfulRAG — the control failed

Its CoT path needs structured-output support that open weights do not provide, so only the ablated wo_cot variant runs. On its own native task the ablated method scores −5.0 against vanilla, below threshold. The method is broken in our hands, so it tells us nothing about transfer. Reported as not reportable, never as defeated.

SHIFT — the mechanism runs and does nothing

The full method runs; three checks confirm the gate fires. The gate effect is +1.3 on n=150 against a 16.2 threshold, and resolving 1.3 points would need n ≈ 23,000. No transfer verdict is reportable, on the same rule.

Two more could not be evaluated at all. One ships a pipeline whose datasets the authors state they cannot release. One requires a four-stage data build whose intermediate format is undocumented. Two execution-stage defenses do not run reliably on any freely-available open-weight model.

This is the availability tax. Reconstruction from released artefacts cost us coverage, and the cost is itself a finding about the field rather than a gap in our work.

We record these as roadblocks, with the reason, rather than quietly dropping the method from the roster.

The distinction matters and a reviewer will test it: FaithfulRAG's control failed because the method was ABLATED, SHIFT's full method runs and simply has no measurable effect here. Different reasons, same verdict, and we separate them. The rule that produces both verdicts is the first of the four: if a defense cannot reproduce its own published benefit in our harness, we report no transfer number from it. Without that rule we would have published two transfer failures that were really harness failures. This is also the honest answer to 'why only ten defenses?' Nine of the seventy-odd candidates in the cell were reachable at all.
