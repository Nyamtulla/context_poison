An attack is a published technique for building content and getting it into the context. A payload is the passage itself, which is what our harness plants. Every one of these is somebody else’s — no item set and no attack in this deck is ours.

All dosed identically: the planted passage replaces 10%, 50% and 100% of the context, with a clean arm at 0%. Every payload is fingerprinted in the result file.

We plant the passage rather than run the retriever, so the retrieval runs measure the generator, not the retriever.

Terminology, because it will be asked. An ATTACK is a published technique: how content is constructed and how it reaches the context. A PAYLOAD is the planted passage itself. We take the released passage and insert it at a fixed dose rather than running the retriever end to end, so these experiments measure what a generator does with poisoned context, not how hard a retriever is to poison. That limit is in the paper's scope paragraph. Knowledge conflict is the odd one out: it is a safety-regime failure mode rather than an attack, and the payload is a field that ships inside the benchmark. Red is the security regime, blue is safety, as everywhere else.
