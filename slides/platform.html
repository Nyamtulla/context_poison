We fixed four rules before any run

The control fires first

A defense that cannot reproduce its own published benefit reports no transfer number. One defense failed this gate.

The attack works first

Every defended arm sits beside an undefended arm measured on the same items, doses and scorer.

Minimum detectable change

α=.05, power=.80, per run. Below it we say “no resolvable effect”, never “no effect”.

Predictions committed

Written into the runner and into git before the run. Several were wrong, and we report which.

Nothing is reimplemented. Every attack and defense runs from its authors’ released code at a pinned commit; 20 repositories verified unmodified. Every item set belongs to the method’s own authors.

Three arms every time: clean dose, undefended, and — in the retrieval cell — no-retrieval. The third is the one no paper in this family reports, and it changed two of our conclusions.

1 × RTX A6000 48GB · Llama-3-8B, Mistral-7B, Qwen2.5-14B · greedy decoding throughout, verified byte-identical on rerun

Each rule answers one of Carlini's criticisms of the DeepSec platform, which is the standing objection to anyone who builds an evaluation platform. Greedy decoding everywhere means there is no run-to-run noise to average over. We checked rather than assumed: one published cell rerun three times from scratch gave byte-identical output. If asked about environments: ACD pins transformers 4.44 and CK-PLUG ships its own 4.49 fork, so the defenses in a single taxonomy cell cannot share one Python environment. That is a reproducibility finding, not just a config note.
