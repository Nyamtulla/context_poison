\section{Recommendations}
\label{sec:discussion}

This section turns the results into practice: the controls an evaluation
should report, the conventions for what a paper claims and tests, what the
released artifacts showed, and what a deployment inherits. Most of it
addresses both communities at once.

For the mechanism that removes evidence rather than planting a lie, the
campaign finds no difference between an optimized passage and an ordinary
retrieval error. For that mechanism the two communities study the same
problem, and the recommendations below treat it as one.

\subsection{Controls both communities should report}

Section~\ref{sec:controls} reports one result that reads the other way
without a no-document score, and Section~\ref{sec:distractor} one that reads
the other way when only accuracy is reported. Neither measurement is
expensive. We propose three reporting requirements.

\textbf{Report a no-document score.} Any fix that modifies how a model uses
retrieved context must report what the same model scores with no retrieved
context at all. Without it, a fix that has stopped reading the document is
indistinguishable from a fix that has learned to resist a poisoned one.
It costs one extra pass over the data.

\textbf{Report where the answers went, not only accuracy.} Under a planted
third answer, ParamMute moves accuracy by less than its threshold while the share of answers
copied from the attacker rises by \EPmDistCopyGain{} points. Accuracy alone
reports that fix as harmless. Classifying each output as the true
answer, the model's own answer, the planted answer or a refusal costs nothing
beyond reading the generations already produced.

\textbf{Report at least two base models.} Our second-model check left the
results unchanged within the range the attack authors evaluate, but it covers
one fix design. A result on one model cannot show that it holds on
another, so a single-model result should be read as specific to that model.

\subsection{Conventions for measurable transfer}

The controls above concern one experiment. These five concern what a paper
claims and what it tests.

\textbf{State the regime the evaluation actually covers.} Our census of
fixes codes each one by what its own paper evaluated, and
\PctOneRegime{}\% cover one regime. One sentence naming the regime the
experiments cover lets a reader, and the maintainer of a system, see that
limit without inferring it.

\textbf{Gate claims on a detectability threshold.} Reporting a difference
without the smallest difference the experiment could have detected makes "no
effect" and "we could not tell" look identical. We computed this for every run
and several of our own results moved from the first category to the second.
The same holds for a claim that something was never tested: it should state
whether it means not done or not resolved.

\textbf{Say whether the attack was adapted to the fix.} A fix that
holds against an attack built for a different target has been shown to resist
that attack and nothing more. In our coding, the context-reliance methods we
ran were validated only against incidental failure, and when we built an
adaptive attack against the one that had resisted, it removed
\PctAcdRemovedGradient{}\% of that method's protection
(Section~\ref{sec:adaptive}). One sentence naming whether the attacker knew
the fix tells a reader which kind of claim they are reading.

\textbf{Compose across the boundary, and measure the composition.}
Section~\ref{sec:parallel} shows the two communities defending different
stages of one pipeline, and Section~\ref{sec:results} shows the reasoning
stage failing on its own once an adversary writes the input. The next step
is to put an ingestion-stage filter in front of a
reasoning-stage method and see whether the pair holds where each half does
not. We do not know that it works. This paper reports no result for a
composed fix; the experiment has started and is not finished. A filter
that passes a fluent falsehood would leave the reasoning stage where our
results find it. Both halves are published with released code, and we found
no paper in the corpus that combines them.

\textbf{Test each fix against the other regime's mechanisms.} A
method aimed at stale or conflicting retrieval is evaluated on
average-case benchmarks, which is the right choice for the problem it was
built for and says nothing about the worst case. Without such a test, a
reader cannot tell whether the reported benefit holds under attack, and our
results show that it can reverse. Running one published poisoning attack
against an existing evaluation needs no new data. The converse holds for a
defense built against an attacker. Its evaluation bounds how many passages
the attacker controls, and an honest mistake is not bound by that number:
RobustRAG turns harmful once half the retrieved passages are stale
(Section~\ref{sec:neither}). A stale-document set needs only a retrieval
pool and a list of answers that changed. A method that cannot be tested in
the other regime should state which regime its benefit holds in.

\subsection{Artifacts}
\label{sec:artifacts}

The reconstruction standard made artifact quality measurable as a side effect.
Among the best-cited untested fixes in the cell we studied, two name
empty repositories. Two more ship code without the trained weights it needs.
One runs. Two further execution-stage defenses do not
run reliably on any freely-available open-weight model. One more,
FaithfulRAG, needs structured-output support
that open weights do not provide in its harness. The ablated variant that
does run shows none of its own benefit, so we report it as not reportable
rather than as defeated.

We report this as a property of the artifacts, not of the methods. A fix
that others cannot run is unlikely to be tested by others, so part of the
coverage gap this paper measures is an artifact gap. It is only part of it.
\NFixUnpaired{} of \NFixes{} fixes never appear in a
confirmed pair, and they are of two kinds. \NFixUnpairedAttacked{} were run
against an adversary whose attack carries no name the literature reuses, which
pairing cannot see. \NDefEvalNoAdversary{} were never run against an adversary
at all. The second group is the larger one, and it is the field rather than
the measurement. \NDefNoAdvSafety{} of those \NDefEvalNoAdversary{} are
safety-regime fixes, demonstrated by accuracy rising on cases where the
context is wrong, and they are the population this paper's campaign probes.
Of the rest, \NDefEvalNoBaseline{} ran no real comparison and the records of
\NDefEvalUnclear{} do not say what they were validated against.

\subsection{Implications for deployment}

Three practical points follow from the results.

A method that tunes how much the model trusts retrieved text is correct
when the text is merely stale and dangerous when an attacker owns it. If the
corpus an agent retrieves from is one an outsider can write to, the second
case is the deployed case.

Consider a system that uses a knowledge-conflict method to improve answer
quality and retrieves from such a corpus. The method operates outside the
regime its authors validated, and our measurements say it is more likely to
increase damage than to reduce it: ParamMute is
worth \NDefCellClean{} on a clean context and \NDefCellSat{} once the
context is corrupted, below the undefended model on the same items. The
claim is about a design choice rather than a community. A method that
converts the document to weights has no dial to turn and does not show the
reversal, and one method in the family resisted until an adaptive attacker
lost most of its protection to an adaptive attacker.

A fix that holds against an optimized adversarial passage may fail against
a fluent sentence that simply asserts a plausible falsehood. In our
measurements no fix resisted both. The fluent attack is also cheaper to
produce: it needs no gradient access and no surrogate retriever, and it does at
least as much damage to an undefended model as the optimized passage.
Evaluations built only around optimized passages can overstate protection. Do not call a fix tested on one
payload class robust to context poisoning.

Finally, coverage differs by channel. Supply-chain and cross-modal content
have fewer named fixes than named mechanisms. If an agent accepts third-party
packages, templates or models, few published fixes address that route.
