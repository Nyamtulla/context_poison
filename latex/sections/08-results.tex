\subsection{One dial, and when turning it helps}

These defenses all turn one dial: how far to trust the retrieved document
over what the model already knows. Turning it toward the document is right
when the document is merely out of date. It is dangerous when an attacker
wrote the document. The rest of this subsection supports that.

The family is defined by what it does: a method whose
intervention is a weighting between the context-conditioned and the
memory-conditioned prediction. It is large, and it is most of what we ran.
Seven of the ten implementations in this cell are in it --- ACD, CK-PLUG,
COIECD, ParamMute, SCR, SpARE and
FaithfulRAG --- as is
SHIFT. Two of them produce no attack result. We ran
SHIFT on clean context only, and FaithfulRAG is the defense
Section~\ref{sec:discussion} reports as not reportable. The published
members we cite are ParamMute,
CK-PLUG, SpARE,
COIECD and SHIFT, all
published as fixes for knowledge conflict and all validated in the safety regime only.

The other three are outside the family and are the reason the claim is not
vacuous. DyPRAG~\cite{tan-2025-dynamic} converts the retrieved passage into weights, so there is no
context-versus-memory dial to turn. The two RobustRAG variants isolate each
passage and aggregate across them, which asks \emph{which} passages agree
rather than how far to trust passages in general. The
family's shared idea is reasonable for the problem it was built for. The
dial runs between \emph{believe the context} and \emph{believe memory}, and
no setting on it separates a real document from a planted one, because both
are context. When a
retrieved document disagrees with the model's memory, the document is usually
newer, so trust the document. The idea becomes a liability the moment the
document is written by an adversary, because the defense instructs the model to
trust the one thing the adversary controls.

\subsection{When the rule does not apply}

The rule above does not say that suppressing the model's memory always
hurts.

It hurts in one case only: when that memory held the right answer. Against BadRAG's denial-of-service variant,
which makes the model refuse rather than answer wrongly, ParamMute is strongly
protective. With one poisoned passage in ten the undefended model refuses
\EPmDosUndefRef\% of the time and holds \EPmDosUndefAcc\% accuracy.
ParamMute drops refusal to \EPmDosDefRef\% and restores accuracy to
\EPmDosDefAcc\%, within \EPmDosCleanGap{} points of its own clean baseline.
Across the realistic range of one to five poisoned passages, the undefended
model averages \EPmDosUndefAvg\% accuracy and ParamMute averages
\EPmDosDefAvg\%.

The mechanism is the same in both cases. ParamMute suppresses the model's
memory. When memory held the correct answer, that suppression destroys it. When
the attack works by driving the model into refusal, suppressing memory removes
the conflict that triggered the refusal. One mechanism, two signs, and what picks the sign is whether the memory was
right. We committed before this run to the prediction that suppressing memory
would do little here. It is why the rule above has a boundary instead of
applying everywhere.

\subsection{Does it take an attacker?}
\label{sec:regime}

Every result in this section is about a defense. This one is not. It is
about the bad text itself. Splitting the corpus into two regimes assumes that
an attacker's passage and an honest mistake do the same damage, and if that
assumption is wrong the split is wrong. Section~\ref{sec:twovocab} makes the
assumption; this measures it.

\input{tables/regime_framing}

Table~\ref{tab:regime} crosses who wrote the payload against what the payload
does. Two rows assert the attacker's false answer in prose written to be
believed. One asserts \emph{the same false answer} using the item's own
retrieved passage with the gold value replaced, which is what a superseded
document looks like. Two rows assert nothing and displace the evidence
instead, one an optimized artifact and one a real passage retrieved for a
different question. Dose, slot positions and scorer are fixed across all of
them. Predictions were registered before the run.

The published construction's repetition buys nothing. PoisonedRAG repeats
its passage five times in each slot it takes. Placed once it loses at most
\NRegimeRepeatMax{}\pp{} of adoption, under threshold at every dose, so no
number in this campaign needs a repetition caveat.

Craft buys an attacker a real advantage, and it buys it at every dose. With
one poisoned passage in ten, the crafted passage is adopted
\PctCraftLow{}\% of the time against \PctStaleLow{}\% for the stale document,
a gap of \NRegimeGapLow{}\pp{}. The gap never falls below
\NRegimeGapMin{}\pp{} anywhere in the range, so it clears the
\NRegimeThresh{}\pp{} threshold at every dose. Writing the sentence to be
believed is worth something, and the campaign can measure it.

\textbf{A document nobody wrote as an attack still carries the model onto the
false answer \PctStaleTop{}\% of the time}, against \PctStaleClean{}\% on a
clean context. It also costs more accuracy than the crafted passage does at
that dose, \AccStaleTop{} against \AccCraftTop{}. The attacker optimizes for
his own answer, not for damage.

Against displacement the adversary buys nothing at all. Zhong et
al.'s optimized artifact and a passage retrieved for someone else's question
land on the same numbers: \PctCorpusTop{}\% and \PctMisretrievalTop{}\%
adoption, \AccCorpusTop{} and \AccMisretrievalTop{} accuracy, every gap inside
the threshold at every dose. For this mechanism, an attack and a retrieval error are the
same event.

Two readings follow, and they are different sizes. The narrow one is a scope
condition on this campaign. The payloads here are not interchangeable with
ordinary wrong documents, at any dose we report. A defense that handles one
has not been shown to handle the other. The broad one holds for one mechanism and not the other. Where the attack
removes the evidence rather than planting a lie, an optimized passage and an
ordinary retrieval error are the same event, and no dose separates them.

Every arm falls below the no-retrieval row by the last dose. Past some dose,
whatever wrote the context has taken more from the model than retrieval gives
it, and the model would do better not reading at all.

\subsection{The shape of the passage, not its name}

The two subsections above hold the payload fixed and vary the defense. The
three below hold the defense fixed and vary the payload, starting with the one
method that resisted. ACD produced the strongest defensive result in our
campaign. It discounts tokens the context makes uncertain, and against corpus
poisoning it is the only method that both reads a clean document and declines a
poisoned one. On a corrupted context it is +\EAcdSatGain\pp above the undefended arm
($p < 0.0001$) and on clean context it is +\EAcdCleanVsNoRet\pp above
answering with no document at all.

That result is not a robustness claim. The corpus-poisoning passage is Zhong et
al.'s released artefact, optimized against a \emph{retriever} and against no
defense of ours. It is therefore oblivious to ACD. A defense evaluated only against attacks
that were not built for it has been shown to resist those attacks and nothing
more, so inferring robustness from this arm would be invalid.

So we claim only what the arm supports: \emph{ACD resists this particular
non-adaptive optimized passage.} An attacker who knew ACD's entropy rule and
optimized against it is untested here. We expect such an attacker to do
better, because the rule is public and differentiable.

The caveat applies to this result alone. By the fourth rule of
Section~\ref{sec:experiments}, a non-adaptive attack that defeats a defense
is a lower bound on attack strength, so every other result in this section is
unaffected.

We then ran the same defense, same items, same doses, against false evidence.

The protection collapses, and Table~\ref{tab:acd} is where. Against the
incoherent payload, ACD restores the no-retrieval floor exactly. Against the fluent payload it ends up
resolvably \emph{below} that floor: the defended model does worse than a model
given no document at all.

Swapping the payload is the cheapest available step toward a worst-case
evaluation, and one step was enough to remove the protection entirely. A
defense whose guarantee does not survive a change of payload class would not be
expected to survive an attacker optimizing against its rule.

The reason is visible in the method. ACD keys on the uncertainty a passage
induces. An optimized token string is incoherent and raises uncertainty, so ACD
discounts it. A fluent sentence asserting a plausible wrong answer lowers
uncertainty, so ACD trusts it more.

This has a practical consequence. The cheaper and more natural-looking
attack is the stronger one. For COIECD's undefended arm, false evidence cuts
accuracy roughly twice as far as gradient-optimized corpus poisoning: a
corrupted context leaves \ECoiecdFalseCorrupt\% against
\ECoiecdCorpusCorrupt\% for corpus poisoning. The
field's attention to optimized adversarial passages is not matched by their
effectiveness.

\EParamFalsePmDrop\pp under false evidence. The reason is structural. Under corpus poisoning the wrong
answer has one source, the model's memory, so inhibiting memory removes it.
Under false evidence it has two, memory and the planted passage, and ParamMute

channel the defense does not operate on.

CK-PLUG is harmful against one payload and flat against the other, and

constraint should do exactly that when the planted passage agrees with the
prior rather than contradicting it. The signal sits below the threshold, so
we record it as directional and not as a result.

\subsection{A ranking we refuse to make}

The three results invite a ranking: which passage is worse for a defense.
Before running them we wrote down the one result that would prove our
explanation wrong. If a defense did \emph{better} against false evidence than
against corpus poisoning, then ``these defenses fail because they trust the
context'' could not be the reason. CK-PLUG does, at +\ECkExcessFalse\pp
against +\ECkExcessCorpus\pp.

That reading does not survive inspection. The two payloads are not equally strong on the
undefended arm. False evidence is roughly twice as damaging: on a corrupted context
CK-PLUG's undefended arm holds \ECkUndefFalseCorrupt\% against
\ECkUndefCorpusCorrupt\%, and COIECD's \ECoiecdFalseCorrupt\% against
\ECoiecdCorpusCorrupt\%. Excess damage is a difference of two collapses, and when
the undefended arm has already collapsed there is less room left for a defense
to make things worse.

Excess-damage comparisons across payload classes are therefore
confounded by attack strength and should not be read as a ranking. Within a
payload, against the control measured beside it in the same run, every
comparison in this section stands.

\subsection{Did it remember, or did it copy?}
\label{sec:distractor}

This subsection answers RQ3. Under false evidence the planted passage asserts the same string the model
would have recalled, so the parametric-answer rate cannot distinguish a model
that remembered from one that copied. They produce identical output. That is the attack's design rather than a flaw in the metric, but it leaves
the central claim of this section resting on an ambiguous measurement.

Planting the \emph{gold} answer does not fix it, because gold is already in the
real context, so an output of gold stays two-ways ambiguous. A third answer
does. We built a payload that plants a fluent sentence asserting an answer that
is neither the gold nor the model's prior, by substituting a distractor into
the item's own evidence sentence so the register is unchanged. Distractors are
drawn from another item's parametric answer, matched on the question's wh-word
and on digit count for numerics, so a person replaces a person and a year
replaces a year. All three answers are checked disjoint per item at build
time, so one output has three disjoint readings. Gold means the model read the
real context. The parametric answer means it recalled from memory. The
distractor means it copied the planted passage.

Two controls license the reading. At clean dose the distractor rate is
\EDistCleanBase\% undefended and \EDistCleanDef\% defended, so the answer
essentially never appears unless it is planted. In the run that carries one, a
no-retrieval arm holds at \EDistCleanNoRet\%:
a model with no document never emits it.

ParamMute succeeds at its stated job and hands the freed probability
mass to the attacker. On a corrupted context it suppresses parametric recall from
\EPmDistPmBase\% to \EPmDistPmDef\%, a \EPmDistPmDrop\pp{} reduction,
resolvable at every dose. Accuracy against the gold answer does not rise with
it; it moves \EPmDistGoldBase\% to \EPmDistGoldDef\%, under the
\EPmDistMdr\pp{} threshold. The copy rate rises \EPmDistCopyBase\% to
\EPmDistCopyDef\%, a \EPmDistCopyGain\pp{} gain that tracks the suppression
almost one for one. This also settles the
measurement limit above: ParamMute's residual parametric rate under false
evidence was copying, not surviving memory.

ACD succeeds at the opposite job and the mass goes to memory instead.
ACD cuts the copy rate from \EAcdDistCopyBase\% to \EAcdDistCopyDef\% on a
corrupted context ($p = 2\times 10^{-40}$, $n=\NAcdAdaptiveItems{}$), the largest defensive effect anywhere in this campaign and
in the direction a defense is supposed to move. The refused mass does not go to
the real document. Parametric recall rises \EAcdDistPmBase\% to \EAcdDistPmDef\% while gold
accuracy moves \EAcdDistGoldBase\% to \EAcdDistGoldDef\%, which clears
Fisher but not the \EAcdDistMdr\pp{} threshold, and sits below the
no-retrieval arm's \EAcdDistNoRet\%. Resisting the attack
did not make the pipeline better than switching retrieval off.

The two results are one mechanism read from both ends. ParamMute
suppresses memory and the attacker fills the vacancy; ACD suppresses the
attacker and memory fills it. Neither moves probability mass toward the real
document, because neither mechanism can tell the real document from the planted
one. Both only choose between the \emph{document} and the model's
\emph{memory}, and the attack is inside the document. So the problem is not
that these defenses trust the document too much. It is that their one choice
cannot separate a real document from a planted one, which is the only
separation that would help.

\input{tables/distractor}

Two more defenses make it a pattern rather than a pair.
Table~\ref{tab:distractor} adds CK-PLUG and COIECD on the same payload and the
same dosing. CK-PLUG behaves as ParamMute does, and more sharply. It is the
only defense in this campaign whose accuracy loss on this payload clears its
own threshold. On a corrupted context it copies \ECkDistCopyDefCorrupt\% against its
undefended arm's \ECkDistCopyBaseCorrupt\%, close to double. COIECD moves in the
same direction at every dose and clears nothing, which is its behavior on both
other payloads too.

The mass is conserved in one direction only. The three outcomes are
disjoint by construction, so the deltas can be summed, and the residual column
reports what is left. For the three defenses that suppress memory, the memory
lost and the copying gained cancel to within 8\pp{}. For CK-PLUG the residual
is exactly its accuracy loss with the sign reversed. These methods do
not create or destroy correctness; they relocate it, out of the model's
parameters and into whatever owns the context.

The defenses that push the other way do not balance. ACD, SCR and SpARE all
move mass from the attacker back toward memory, and all three lose a large
share of it on the way: $\EAcdResid$, $\EScrResid$ and $\ESpareResid$\pp{}
leave the three-way partition altogether. In that fraction of cases the output is none of gold,
memory or the distractor.

The direction of the dial predicts whether mass is conserved. Turn it
toward the context and the accounting closes. Turn it toward memory and a sixth
to a quarter of the sample goes somewhere the partition cannot see. The
residual is a property of the direction rather than of one implementation:
SCR reaches almost the same figure by a different route, intervening by
prompting where ACD intervenes by decoding. SpARE, which steers activations, loses more still. Whether
the lost mass is abstention or degradation is not a question this payload can
answer. We flag it as the next measurement on this arm rather than read it
either way.

SpARE's row needs one caution. Its run carries no undefended arm, so it compares
steering toward memory against steering toward the context. That is a comparison
between two settings of the same dial, not between a defense and its absence.

\subsection{The one defense that expects an attacker}

SCR is the only method we ran whose design anticipates
an adversary. It shows the model its own no-context answer, the document, and
the answer the document supports, and asks the model to judge which to believe.

It does help on a corrupted context, +\EScrSatGain\pp over the undefended arm
($p = 0.025$), and it does genuinely read the document, scoring
\EScrCleanVsNoRet\pp above no-retrieval on clean context. It pays
\EScrCleanCost\pp on a clean context to buy that \EScrSatGain{}. The cost is
resolvable and is roughly five times the benefit. A defense deployed permanently and attacked occasionally is a poor
trade at that exchange rate.

SCR's parametric-answer rate climbs from \EScrPmClean{} on a clean
context to \EScrPmSat{} on a corrupted one, moving toward the closed-book
rate of \EScrNoRetPm{}. Both SCR and ACD slide from
the document toward memory as the attack grows. SCR simply destroys more
correct answers on the way.

\subsection{Results that flip without a control}
\label{sec:controls}

Each of the following reverses if the arm beside it is dropped, and in each
case that arm is one the relevant literature does not report.

\textbf{A no-retrieval baseline.} DyPRAG is perfectly
flat under an attack that destroys the undefended arm, scoring
\EDypragClean\% on a clean context and \EDypragSat\% on a corrupted one, across
the dose range while the undefended arm falls from \EDypragRagClean\% to
\EDypragRagSat\%. Read alone, that is the strongest defense result in the
campaign. The closed-book arm scores \EDypragNoRet\% and is statistically
indistinguishable from it.
DyPRAG is flat because it is not reading the document, not because it
is resisting the attack. Only a no-retrieval arm separates immunity from
inattention, and no paper in this family reports one.

\textbf{An abstention axis.} COIECD is inert on accuracy at every dose. Dumping
and classifying every generation shows it is not inert at all. On the items
where the undefended model answered from memory, COIECD converts
32.1\% of those answers into explicit abstentions, and does so only
under attack; on clean context the same pairing moves almost nothing. Because
the parametric answer is wrong by construction in this data, what it suppressed
were confident wrong answers and what it produced instead was "not mentioned".
That is a real safety gain, and it is invisible in the accuracy column that
every paper in this family reports.

\textbf{A model control.} RobustRAG ran on Mistral and the
context-reliance family ran on Llama, because those are the models each
paper released. Re-running RobustRAG on the other family's model changes
outcomes. The tone-steering finding survives unchanged, which strengthens
it. The implementation divergence below does not. At nine poisoned passages
against refusal induction, KeywordAgg is \NKeywordNineMistral{}\pp{} on
Mistral and \NKeywordNineLlama{}\pp{} on Llama: the variant that is
resolvably protective on one model is not protective on the other, while
DecodingAgg stays harmful on both (\NDecodingNineMistral{}\pp{} and
\NDecodingNineLlama{}\pp{}). Holding defense and attack fixed and changing
only the base model changes an effect's size, its existence, and in one case
its sign.

\subsection{Is it the design, or one codebase?}

A single implementation cannot tell you whether you have found a property of a
design principle or a quirk of one codebase. RobustRAG
ships two aggregation variants that share the isolate-then-aggregate principle
and share no decision logic downstream of it. One extracts keywords and
re-prompts; the other averages next-token distributions.

Both show the same behavior under tone steering, at or below 0.3\% across
four cells and two models, against CK-PLUG's 99.0\% on the same attack and
model. Aggregation does not open the tone-steering hole; context-reliance
does. Two implementations that share only the principle agree here, so the
behavior belongs to the principle.

The caution runs the other way too. Across fifteen dose levels the two
implementations agree within noise on thirteen. On one they do not. At nine
poisoned passages in ten, against refusal induction, KeywordAgg is
\NKeywordNineMistral{}\pp{} and DecodingAgg is
\NDecodingNineMistral{}\pp{}, with both ends clearing the \ERobustMdr\pp
threshold at $n=300$. One implementation of isolate-then-aggregate is
resolvably protective and the other is resolvably harmful, on the same
attack, at the same dose, inside one codebase --- on this model. The control
above shows the protective end does not survive a change of base model, so
the divergence is a property of the pair, not of the principle.

The gap grows as the honest minority of passages shrinks: 13.0 points at five
in ten, 29.7 at nine in ten. It then collapses to 1.7 at ten in ten, where no
honest passage remains and the two implementations have nothing left to
disagree about. A survey reporting one number per published method would have
missed all of this.

\subsection{Every defense against every attack}
\label{sec:grid}

Everything above runs on CoConflictQA, where an item carries one context
passage and the only two conditions are a clean context and a corrupted one.
The campaign also runs every defense against every attack on open Natural
Questions, where a dose is $j$ of the top ten retrieved passages.
Table~\ref{tab:grid} is that grid: \NGridCells{} cells, each read against
its own undefended arm on the same items at the same dose. The two item sets
are not comparable and we do not compare them. What the grid adds is breadth:
the design rule above was read off one attack, and
here it meets four.

Across that breadth almost nothing helps. Of the \NGridCells{} cells,
\NGridGainTop{} show the defended arm ahead at the top dose by more than
that run's detectability threshold, \NGridLoseTop{} show it behind by more
than the threshold, and \NGridFlatTop{} resolve to no difference. All
\NGridGainTop{} gains are against the denial-of-service payload, where the
undefended model refuses to answer and any method that restores an answer
gains accuracy for a reason that is not robustness. Every cell that resolves
against a payload which plants a false answer rather than suppressing one
resolves downward.

The losses are concentrated the way that rule predicts.
CK-PLUG and DyPRAG hold \NGridLoseTopTwo{} of the \NGridLoseTop{}: both
suppress the model's own parameters to force reliance on retrieved text, and
both pay for it on every payload they meet.

The clean column prices them. \NGridFreeClean{} of the \NGridCells{} cells
cost nothing measurable when no passage is planted, so most of these methods
are free to leave switched on. The \NGridCostClean{} that are not belong to
the same two methods, and both pay more than twenty points before any
passage is planted.

Two mechanisms in this grid are not yet complete. A stale document and a
diluted window are running, and we report no numbers for them here.

\subsection{The defenses that do transfer}
\label{sec:transfer}

The exceptions are informative. RobustRAG stops PoisonedRAG, which its own
paper tested, and we reproduce that (+\ERobustPoisonedGain{}\pp). Against
corpus poisoning it neither helps nor hurts anywhere in the range we report:
\ERobustDispOneDef{}\% against the undefended arm's \ERobustDispOneUndef{}\%
at one displaced passage and \ERobustDispFiveDef{}\% against
\ERobustDispFiveUndef{}\% at five, both gaps inside the
\ERobustDispMdr{}\pp{} threshold. Refilling the window from ranks the
attacker does not hold moves it to \ERobustBackfillFive{}\% at five, also
inside the threshold.

One condition past that range says why, and we report it as a diagnostic
rather than as a result. When the attacker holds every retrieved passage both
arms sit on the floor, \ERobustDispTenDef{}\% and
\ERobustDispTenUndef{}\%, and the same refill restores
\ERobustBackfillTen{}\%. What decides that cell is not the defense but the
attacker's budget against the retrieval depth. Isolate-then-aggregate needs
an honest passage to aggregate, and whether one exists is a property of the
pipeline rather than of the method. The defense's own authors sweep a
corruption size up to half the window and no further, so we draw no verdict
about the defense from a condition they do not evaluate.

The same defense meets the other regime twice, and the two cells disagree.
Against distractor evidence --- a passage asserting a wrong answer, which is
what RobustRAG's threat model describes with the attacker taken out --- it
holds at low dose. At two planted passages in ten the keyword variant scores
\ERobustDistKeyTwo{}\% and the decoding variant \ERobustDistDecTwo{}\%,
against the undefended arm's \ERobustDistUndefTwo{}\%. The hold does not
survive dosing: at ten in ten both sit near \ERobustDistKeyTen{}\% against
\ERobustDistUndefTen{}\%. Against misretrieval it never helps. Both defended
arms start below the undefended one, \ERobustMisKeyClean{}\% and
\ERobustMisDecClean{}\% against \ERobustMisUndefClean{}\%, and both are
still below it at five in ten, \ERobustMisKeyFive{}\% and
\ERobustMisDecFive{}\% against \ERobustMisUndefFive{}\%. Misretrieval
plants nothing, so isolate-then-aggregate has no dishonest passage to isolate
and the defense pays its accuracy cost for nothing.

A defense built for an adversary transfers on the shape of the stimulus, not
on the presence of one. Where an incidental mechanism puts a wrong claim in
the window it is worth running. Where it only degrades what the retriever
returns it is a cost.

Across the agent cell, \NAgentHolds{} of \NAgentConditions{} conditions show
the technique holding, with attack success driven to zero. A further 4 show a
resolvable reduction. The rest divide between 19 with no resolvable effect at
that sample size and 9 with no reduction at all.

Every hold is at ingestion or at execution. Of the
\NAgentHolds{} that hold, \NAgentHoldsIngestion{} act at ingestion and
\NAgentHoldsExecution{} at execution, which is every execution-stage
technique we ran. None of the \NAgentTriedReasoning{}
reasoning-stage conditions holds. The stage a technique acts at, rather than
the technique, predicts whether it survives here --- the division
Section~\ref{sec:parallel} finds in the literature, reproduced in what the
methods actually do.

\subsection{The dose that favors a defense most}
\label{sec:lowdose}

A result read at the top of the dose range is read where the attack is
strongest and the measurement is weakest. The fewer honest passages remain,
the more a low accuracy measures the attack rather than a failing defense.

So we report the other end as well, and the attacks supply it. One poisoned
passage in ten is the default in the released attack constructor, and it is
the dose most favorable to a defense: nine honest passages remain for it to
work with. If a method can discount a planted passage while still using a
good one, this is where it shows.

\NGridLowHelps{} of the \NGridLowPairs{} pairs in Table~\ref{tab:grid} show
a resolvable benefit there. Every other defense is at or below its own
undefended arm, at the dose that favors it most, against an attack run at
its authors' own strength. Both exceptions are the denial-of-service payload,
where the undefended model refuses to answer and any method that restores an
answer gains accuracy for a reason that is not robustness.

The table is also the worst-case reading that a security claim requires. A
defense beaten by one of three payloads is not two-thirds robust, it is broken,
and averaging across payloads would hide that. We therefore report the
minimum rather than the mean: robustness is a property of the strongest
attack a method has met, not of the attacks its authors chose.

\subsection{An attacker who reads the defense}
\label{sec:adaptive}

This subsection answers RQ5. Every result above comes from an attack that was not built for the defense it
met. Section~\ref{sec:experiments} states why that is conservative for a
failure and invalid for a resistance. ACD is our one resistance result, so it
is the one that has to be tested properly.

ACD mixes the no-context and with-context next-token distributions by
\[
d_\alpha = \frac{H}{H_{\mathrm{ctx}} + H},
\qquad
s \leftarrow s + d_\alpha\,(s_{\mathrm{ctx}} - s),
\]
where $H$ is the Shannon entropy of the no-context distribution and
$H_{\mathrm{ctx}}$ that of the with-context one. $H$ is a property of the
question. $H_{\mathrm{ctx}}$ is conditioned on the retrieved context, which an
attacker who owns the corpus controls outright. So the attack has
exactly one objective: minimize $H_{\mathrm{ctx}}$.

The ceiling follows from the rule, not from an experiment. As
$H_{\mathrm{ctx}} \to 0$, $d_\alpha \to 1$ and the update collapses to
$s_{\mathrm{ctx}}$ unchanged, which is the undefended model. A perfect adaptive
attack of this kind turns ACD into plain RAG and can do no better. It cannot
drive ACD \emph{below} its own undefended arm. $d_\alpha$ is bounded in
$(0,1)$, and the endpoints are closed-book and plain RAG. We committed that
bound, and five further predictions, before building the payload. An ACD arm
landing below the undefended arm would have falsified our account of the
mechanism.

The attack is white-box and query-based. Five candidate passages per item, all
asserting the \emph{same} distractor answer and differing only in form, each
scored by the $H_{\mathrm{ctx}}$ ACD itself would compute, keeping the
minimum.

The attacker optimizes on the items it then attacks. This is not training on
the test set. The threat model gives the adversary the model and the defense rule, and an
adversary who owns a corpus tunes the passage they are about to plant. A payload
optimized on held-out items and transferred would measure something else, and
something weaker. What is held out is the few-shot block: its demonstrations
are drawn from items outside the evaluation slice and are never poisoned, so
the defense is not shown the attack in its own prompt. Holding the asserted answer fixed is what makes any movement
attributable to form rather than to a different claim. It is a deliberately
weak optimizer, so what it achieves is again a lower bound.

All six predictions held, and Table~\ref{tab:adaptive} carries
them. $H_{\mathrm{ctx}}$ falls 42\%, $d_\alpha$ rises toward 1, ACD's copy rate rises from \EAcdDistCopyDef\% to
\EAcdAdaptCopy\% ($p = 9.5\times10^{-18}$), its parametric recall falls
toward the undefended arm's, and its accuracy lands on \EAcdAdaptAcc\% ---
exactly the undefended arm, and below the no-retrieval floor of
\EAcdAdaptNoRet\%. ACD does not fall below the
undefended arm on copy rate, as the ceiling requires.

The honest size of the effect is the gap, not ACD's rise. The adaptive
payload is stronger against the undefended arm as well, so reporting only
ACD's change would credit the defense with damage the attack does to everyone.
Against its own undefended arm the protection falls from \NAcdGapBase{}\pp{}
to \NAcdGapBestOf{}\pp{}: \PctAcdRemovedBestOf{}\% of it removed by a
best-of-five search over sentence templates. Not all of it. $d_\alpha$ reached 0.653 of a possible 1.0, which
left a question the weak optimizer could not answer.

A gradient search answers it. We ran a GCG-style~\cite{zou-2023-universal} coordinate search
over a 20-token suffix against the same objective, appended to the passage
best-of-five had already chosen so that the asserted answer stays fixed and
any movement is the gradient's. Candidate tokens are restricted to
space-leading printable ASCII, because a suffix that does not survive a
decode and re-encode is not a passage an attacker can place in a corpus; the
round trip is exact on all \NAcdAdaptiveItems{} items. A gradient passage is kept only where it
beats the best-of-five passage on the reported measure, which it fails to do
on 163 of \NAcdAdaptiveItems{}, so the payload is monotone by construction.

It drives $H_{\mathrm{ctx}}$ to 0.614 and $d_\alpha$ to 0.708, and the
defensive gap falls to \NAcdGapGradient{}\pp{}: \PctAcdRemovedGradient{}\% of ACD's protection removed,
twice what sentence templates took. The \NAcdGradientBeyond{}\pp{} it takes beyond
best-of-five clears the \NAcdAdaptiveMdr{}\pp{} detectability threshold at this
sample size.

The gradient payload is weaker in general and stronger against the
defense. It damages the undefended arm \emph{less} than best-of-five
does, \EAcdGradUndefCopy\% against \EAcdAdaptUndefCopy\%, while taking
considerably more from ACD. The gap
is what isolates the defense, and the gap is what moved. The search therefore attacks the method rather than simply producing a
better payload.

The two conditions agree. On a clean context the three payloads are
indistinguishable: no gap between the arms exceeds \NAcdGapCleanMax{}\pp{},
because there is nothing there to defend against. They separate only once
the context is corrupted, which is what one expects of a rule keyed on a
statistic computed over the whole context.

ACD degrades under adaptive pressure; it does not collapse. We
committed the bands in advance: below 10\pp{} of remaining gap we would have
called it neutralized, and 10 to 25\pp{} degraded further while holding
something back. The result is the second. A rule that gives up
\PctAcdRemovedGradient{}\% of its
protection to an attacker with gradients is a real obstacle that has now been
priced, rather than either a solved problem or a broken one.

What this licenses is still narrow. It does not show ACD is broken, and the
ceiling in the rule says no attack of this kind could have driven it below the
undefended arm. One defense in our campaign looked like a counterexample. It
gives up most of its protection to an attacker who reads the method. Every
published evaluation of a context-reliance defense in our corpus is
non-adaptive. The gap between non-adaptive and adaptive
evaluation is large enough to change the verdict.
