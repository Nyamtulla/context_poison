\subsection{A shared design rule}

A defense that works by shifting trust from the model's parameters toward
the retrieved text is correct when the text is merely stale, and dangerous
when an attacker owns it. The rest of this subsection supports that.

The family is defined by what it does: a method whose
intervention is a weighting between the context-conditioned and the
memory-conditioned prediction. It is large, and it is most of what we ran.
Seven of the ten implementations in this cell are in it --- ACD, CK-PLUG,
COIECD, ParamMute, SCR, SpARE and
FaithfulRAG --- as is
SHIFT. Two of them produce no attack result. We ran
SHIFT on clean context only, and FaithfulRAG is the defense
Section~\ref{sec:discussion} reports as not reportable. The published
members we cite are ParamMute,
CK-PLUG, SpARE,
COIECD and SHIFT, all
published as fixes for knowledge conflict and all validated in the safety regime only.

The other three are outside the family and are the reason the claim is not
vacuous. DyPRAG~\cite{tan-2025-dynamic} converts the retrieved passage into weights, so there is no
context-versus-memory dial to turn. The two RobustRAG variants isolate each
passage and aggregate across them, which asks \emph{which} passages agree
rather than how far to trust passages in general. The
family's shared idea is reasonable for the problem it was built for. The
dial runs between \emph{believe the context} and \emph{believe memory}, and
no setting on it separates a real document from a planted one, because both
are context. When a
retrieved document disagrees with the model's memory, the document is usually
newer, so trust the document. The idea becomes a liability the moment the
document is written by an adversary, because the defense instructs the model to
trust the one thing the adversary controls.

SpARE is the cleanest case because its control and its conviction are the same
measurement. Steering toward the context is worth +14.2\pp when the
context is honest and costs -10.2\pp when it is attacked. Both ends
clear the 8.9\pp threshold. The sign flips.

ParamMute shows the same shape on a larger sample. Its benefit on clean context

across the full dose range it loses 13.5\pp more accuracy than doing nothing at

CK-PLUG's behavior is the most extreme we measured, and it appears on a
different metric. Under BadRAG sentiment steering~\cite{xue-2024-badrag}, which

\subsection{The scope condition}

The rule above is not that suppressing parametric memory is always harmful.
We can state when it is.

Suppressing the model's prior knowledge hurts only where that prior
knowledge held the right answer. Against BadRAG's denial-of-service variant,
which makes the model refuse rather than answer wrongly, ParamMute is strongly
protective. With one poisoned passage in ten the undefended model refuses
52.4\% of the time and holds 27.0\% accuracy. ParamMute drops refusal to 0.2\%
and restores accuracy to 57.4\%, within 2.4 points of its own clean baseline.
Across the realistic range of one to five poisoned passages, the undefended
model averages 25.8\% accuracy and ParamMute averages 57.6\%.

The mechanism is the same in both cases. ParamMute suppresses the model's
memory. When memory held the correct answer, that suppression destroys it. When
the attack works by driving the model into refusal, suppressing memory removes
the conflict that triggered the refusal. One mechanism, two signs,
selected by whether the memory was right. The prediction committed before
this run was that suppressing memory would do little here; the result is why
the paper states a scope condition rather than a blanket one.

\subsection{Payload provenance}
\label{sec:regime}

Every result in this section is about a defense. This one is not. The
paper divides its corpus by regime, and that division rests on a claim about
the stimulus rather than about any method: that an adversary and an accident
produce the same failure. Section~\ref{sec:twovocab} states it; this
measures it.

\input{tables/regime_framing}

Table~\ref{tab:regime} crosses who wrote the payload against what the payload
does. Two rows assert the attacker's false answer in prose written to be
believed. One asserts \emph{the same false answer} using the item's own
retrieved passage with the gold value replaced, which is what a superseded
document looks like. Two rows assert nothing and displace the evidence
instead, one an optimized artifact and one a real passage retrieved for a
different question. Dose, slot positions and scorer are fixed across all of
them. Predictions were registered before the run.

The published construction's repetition buys nothing. PoisonedRAG repeats
its passage five times in each slot it takes. Placed once it loses at most
\NRegimeRepeatMax{}\pp{} of adoption, under threshold at every dose, so no
number in this campaign needs a repetition caveat.

Craft buys an attacker a real advantage, and only at low dose. With
one poisoned passage in ten, the crafted passage is adopted
\PctCraftLow{}\% of the time against \PctStaleLow{}\% for the stale document,
a gap of \NRegimeGapLow{}\pp{} that clears the threshold. That is the dose
range a deployment actually faces. At saturation the gap is
\NRegimeGapSat{}\pp{}, inside the threshold, and the two are not
distinguishable. \textbf{A document nobody wrote as an attack still carries
the model onto the false answer \PctStaleSat{}\% of the time.} It also costs
more accuracy at saturation than the crafted passage does, \AccStaleSat{}
against \AccCraftSat{}. The attacker optimizes for his own answer, not for
damage.

Against displacement the adversary buys nothing at all. Zhong et
al.'s optimized artifact and a passage retrieved for someone else's question
land on the same numbers: \PctCorpusSat{}\% and \PctMisretrievalSat{}\%
adoption, \AccCorpusSat{} and \AccMisretrievalSat{} accuracy, every gap inside
the threshold. For this mechanism, an attack and a retrieval error are the
same event.

Two readings follow, and they are different sizes. The narrow one is a scope
condition on this campaign. At low dose the payloads here are not
interchangeable with ordinary wrong documents. A defense that handles one has
not been shown to handle the other. The broad one is about the field. The two
literatures study a failure that is substantially the same stimulus, and
becomes the same stimulus exactly where it hurts most --- under saturation,
and for the mechanism that removes evidence rather than inserting a lie. The
citation gap in Section~\ref{sec:literatures} is therefore not two communities
working on different problems.

Every arm falls below the no-retrieval row by the last dose. Past some dose,
whatever wrote the context has taken more from the model than retrieval gives
it, and the model would do better not reading at all.

\subsection{Payload class, not attack label}

The two subsections above hold the payload fixed and vary the defense. The
three below hold the defense fixed and vary the payload, starting with the one
method that resisted. ACD produced the strongest defensive result in our
campaign. It discounts tokens the context makes uncertain, and against corpus
poisoning it is the only method that both reads a clean document and declines a
poisoned one. At saturation it is +9.2\pp above the undefended arm
($p < 0.0001$) and on clean context it is +53.5\pp above answering
with no document at all.

That result is not a robustness claim. The corpus-poisoning passage is Zhong et
al.'s released artefact, optimized against a \emph{retriever} and against no
defense of ours. It is therefore oblivious to ACD, in the sense
Carlini~\cite{carlini-2019-critique} uses when criticizing the DeepSec
platform. A defense evaluated only against attacks that were not built for it
has been shown to resist those attacks and nothing more. Inferring robustness
from that is invalid. It is the specific error that invalidated DeepSec's
defense rankings.

So we claim only what the arm supports: \emph{ACD resists this particular
non-adaptive optimized passage.} An attacker who knew ACD's entropy rule and
optimized against it is untested here. We expect such an attacker to do
better, because the rule is public and differentiable.

The caveat applies to this result alone. By the fourth rule of
Section~\ref{sec:experiments}, a non-adaptive attack that defeats a defense
is a lower bound on attack strength, so every other result in this section is
unaffected.

We then ran the same defense, same items, same doses, against false evidence.

The protection collapses. Against the incoherent payload, ACD restores
the no-retrieval floor exactly. Against the fluent payload it ends up
resolvably \emph{below} that floor: the defended model does worse than a model
given no document at all.

Swapping the payload is the cheapest available step toward a worst-case
evaluation, and one step was enough to remove the protection entirely. A
defense whose guarantee does not survive a change of payload class would not be
expected to survive an attacker optimizing against its rule.

The reason is visible in the method. ACD keys on the uncertainty a passage
induces. An optimized token string is incoherent and raises uncertainty, so ACD
discounts it. A fluent sentence asserting a plausible wrong answer lowers
uncertainty, so ACD trusts it more.

This has a practical consequence. The cheaper and more natural-looking
attack is the stronger one. For COIECD's undefended arm, false evidence cuts
accuracy roughly twice as far as gradient-optimized corpus poisoning at every
dose, falling to 15.2\% at 10\% dose against 26.5\% for corpus poisoning. The
field's attention to optimized adversarial passages is not matched by their
effectiveness.

each against its own undefended arm over the same items at the same doses.
Predictions for all three were committed to version control before any of them
ran. Table~\ref{tab:payloads} puts the two payload classes side by side.

ParamMute is harmful against both, and the way it fails is the
finding. On this run's honest context it is worth +9.7\pp, against the
+9.3\pp of the corpus-poisoning run beside it. It costs 12 to 13 points more
than doing nothing once an adversary writes the context, and it does not matter
whether the adversary writes gibberish or prose. What does change is how well
ParamMute does its own job. Its suppression of the wrong parametric answer
falls from -42.5\pp under corpus poisoning to -8.8\pp under
false evidence. The reason is structural. Under corpus poisoning the wrong
answer has one source, the model's memory, so inhibiting memory removes it.
Under false evidence it has two, memory and the planted passage, and ParamMute
touches only one of them. The attack duplicated the answer into the
channel the defense does not operate on.

CK-PLUG is harmful against one payload and flat against the other, and
the shape matters more than the sign. Under corpus poisoning its cost grows
with the attack: -4.5\pp on clean context, then -14.9 and -15.1 once poison is
present. Under false evidence it is a constant offset of about -4.4\pp at every
dose including the clean one, and no cell clears the 7.0\pp threshold. CK-PLUG
pays a fixed price for being switched on; corpus poisoning amplifies that price
and false evidence does not.

COIECD is inert against both. Of its eight comparisons the largest is
1.8\pp against a threshold of 9.9\pp, and no $p$-value falls below 0.20. Its memorization-rate
suppression does shrink roughly threefold under false evidence. An entropy
constraint should do exactly that when the planted passage agrees with the
prior rather than contradicting it. The signal sits below the threshold, so
we record it as directional and not as a result.

\subsection{A comparison we exclude}

The three results invite a ranking across payload classes. We committed in
advance to one falsifier. If any defense did \emph{better} against false
evidence than against corpus poisoning, then ``these defenses fail because
they trust the context'' would not be the mechanism. CK-PLUG does, at
+0.4\pp against +5.4\pp.

That reading does not survive inspection. The two payloads are not equally strong on the
undefended arm. False evidence is roughly twice as damaging at every dose: for
CK-PLUG's undefended arm, 23.0\% against 39.2\% at 10\% dose, and for COIECD's,
15.2\% against 26.5\%. Excess damage is a difference of two collapses, and when
the undefended arm has already collapsed there is less room left for a defense
to make things worse.

Excess-damage comparisons across payload classes are therefore
confounded by attack strength and should not be read as a ranking. Within a
payload, against the control measured beside it in the same run, every
comparison in this section stands.

\subsection{Copying versus remembering}
\label{sec:distractor}

This subsection answers RQ3. Under false evidence the planted passage asserts the same string the model
would have recalled, so the parametric-answer rate cannot distinguish a model
that remembered from one that copied. They produce identical output. That is the attack's design rather than a flaw in the metric, but it leaves
the central claim of this section resting on an ambiguous measurement.

Planting the \emph{gold} answer does not fix it, because gold is already in the
real context, so an output of gold stays two-ways ambiguous. A third answer
does. We built a payload that plants a fluent sentence asserting an answer that
is neither the gold nor the model's prior, by substituting a distractor into
the item's own evidence sentence so the register is unchanged. Distractors are
drawn from another item's parametric answer, matched on the question's wh-word
and on digit count for numerics, so a person replaces a person and a year
replaces a year. All three answers are checked disjoint per item at build
time, so one output has three disjoint readings. Gold means the model read the
real context. The parametric answer means it recalled from memory. The
distractor means it copied the planted passage.

Two controls license the reading. At clean dose the distractor rate is 0.8\%
undefended and 0.6\% defended, so the answer essentially never appears unless
it is planted. In the run that carries one, a no-retrieval arm holds at 1.0\%:
a model with no document never emits it.

ParamMute succeeds at its stated job and hands the freed probability
mass to the attacker. At saturation it suppresses parametric recall from
39.0\% to 2.9\%, a 36.1\pp{} reduction, resolvable at every dose. Accuracy
against the gold answer does not rise with it; it moves 5.4\% to 1.5\%, under
the 7.1\pp{} threshold. The copy rate rises 43.9\% to 81.4\%, a 37.5\pp{}
gain that tracks the suppression almost one for one. This also settles the
measurement limit above: ParamMute's residual parametric rate under false
evidence was copying, not surviving memory.

ACD succeeds at the opposite job and the mass goes to memory instead.
ACD cuts the copy rate from 76.0\% to 40.8\% at saturation ($p = 2\times
10^{-40}$, $n=\NAcdAdaptiveItems{}$), the largest defensive effect anywhere in this campaign and
in the direction a defense is supposed to move. The refused mass does not go to
the real document. Parametric recall rises 14.1\% to 32.5\% while gold
accuracy moves 2.3\% to 5.0\%, which clears Fisher but not the 7.6\pp{}
threshold, and sits below the no-retrieval arm's 8.2\%. Resisting the attack
did not make the pipeline better than switching retrieval off.

The two results are one mechanism read from both ends. ParamMute
suppresses memory and the attacker fills the vacancy; ACD suppresses the
attacker and memory fills it. Neither moves probability mass toward the real
document, because neither mechanism can tell the real document from the planted
one. Both only arbitrate between \emph{context} and \emph{parameters}, and the
attack lives inside the context. The problem is
not that context-reliance defenses trust context too much. It is that the axis
they arbitrate on does not contain the distinction an attacker violates.

\input{tables/distractor}

Two more defenses make it a pattern rather than a pair.
Table~\ref{tab:distractor} adds CK-PLUG and COIECD on the same payload and the
same dosing. CK-PLUG behaves as ParamMute does, and more sharply. It is the
only defense in this campaign whose accuracy loss on this payload clears its
own threshold. At a 10\% dose it already copies 76.7\% against its undefended
arm's 38.5\%. That is double the copying, on one tenth of the context. COIECD moves in the
same direction at every dose and clears nothing, which is its behavior on both
other payloads too.

The mass is conserved in one direction only. The three outcomes are
disjoint by construction, so the deltas can be summed, and the residual column
reports what is left. For the three defenses that suppress memory, the memory
lost and the copying gained cancel to within 8\pp{}. For CK-PLUG the residual
is exactly its accuracy loss with the sign reversed. These methods do
not create or destroy correctness; they relocate it, out of the model's
parameters and into whatever owns the context.

The defenses that push the other way do not balance. ACD, SCR and SpARE all
move mass from the attacker back toward memory, and all three lose a large
share of it on the way: $16.8$, $16.5$ and $23.5$\pp{} leave the three-way
partition altogether. In that fraction of cases the output is none of gold,
memory or the distractor.

The direction of the dial predicts whether mass is conserved. Turn it
toward the context and the accounting closes. Turn it toward memory and a sixth
to a quarter of the sample goes somewhere the partition cannot see. The
residual is a property of the direction rather than of one implementation:
SCR reaches almost the same figure by a different route, intervening by
prompting where ACD intervenes by decoding. SpARE, which steers activations, loses more still. Whether
the lost mass is abstention or degradation is not a question this payload can
answer. We flag it as the next measurement on this arm rather than read it
either way.

SpARE's row needs one caution. Its run carries no undefended arm, so it compares
steering toward memory against steering toward the context. That is a comparison
between two settings of the same dial, not between a defense and its absence.

\subsection{An LLM-as-judge arm}

SCR is the only method we ran whose design anticipates
an adversary. It shows the model its own no-context answer, the document, and
the answer the document supports, and asks the model to judge which to believe.

It does help at saturation, +2.8\pp over the undefended arm ($p = 0.025$), and
it does genuinely read the document, scoring 41.2\pp above no-retrieval on
clean context. It pays 13.5 points on clean data to buy that 2.8. The
cost is resolvable at every dose below saturation and is roughly five times the
benefit. A defense deployed permanently and attacked occasionally is a poor
trade at that exchange rate.

SCR's parametric-answer rate climbs 22.8, 33.8, 35.8, 52.5 across the dose
range, converging on the closed-book rate of 55.8. Both SCR and ACD slide from
the document toward memory as the attack grows. SCR simply destroys more
correct answers on the way.
