\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\paragraph{What an attacker gains over an honest mistake.}
\label{sec:regime}
Planting a false claim gives an attacker a measurable
advantage over an honest mistake. Pushing the right evidence out gives no
advantage at all. The research questions below assume that a planted passage
and an honest mistake do comparable damage, so we measured that first, with no
defense in place. Table~\ref{tab:regime} runs five kinds of planted passage on
the same items, with the same number of poisoned passages in the same
positions, scored the same way.

Where the passage plants a false answer, writing it to persuade helps the
attacker. With one poisoned passage in ten, the model gives the planted answer
\PctCraftLow{}\% of the time for PoisonedRAG's crafted passage and
\PctStaleLow{}\% for a real passage whose answer has changed. The gap clears the
detection threshold at every number of poisoned passages, though only
narrowly at two, and at five poisoned passages the crafted passage also
costs more accuracy, \AccCraftTop{}\% against \AccStaleTop{}\%. The honest
mistake still does real damage: it moves the model to the false answer
\PctStaleTop{}\% of the time, against \PctStaleClean{}\% with nothing planted.
Repeating the crafted passage five times, as the published attack does,
changes these rates by at most \NRegimeRepeatMax{} points.

Where the passage only pushes the right evidence out of the retrieved set, the
attacker gains nothing. Zhong et al.'s optimized passage and a real passage
retrieved for another question give the same results: \PctCorpusTop{}\% and
\PctMisretrievalTop{}\% planted answers, \AccCorpusTop{}\% and
\AccMisretrievalTop{}\% accuracy, with every gap below the threshold at every
number of poisoned passages. For this kind of attack, an attack and an
ordinary retrieval error are the same event.

\subsection{RQ2: Does a defense built for one literature's problem survive the other's?}

\paragraph{First direction: safety fixes facing an attacker.}
The methods built for honest error all decide how far
to trust the retrieved document over what the model already knows. Trusting
the document more is right when the document is out of date, and wrong when an
attacker wrote it. Seven of the ten methods we ran on retrieved documents
work this way: ACD, CK-PLUG, COIECD, ParamMute, SCR, SpARE and FaithfulRAG.
None of them uses any signal that separates a real document from a planted
one. When the document is honest and newer than the model's memory, leaning
on it helps. When an attacker wrote it, leaning on it moves the model toward
the attacker's answer.

Table~\ref{tab:family} shows the sign change. SpARE changes accuracy by
+\ESpareClean{} points on a clean context and by \ESpareSat{} on a corrupted
one, both beyond its threshold. ParamMute gains \EParamClean{} points on a clean context
and ends \EParamSatGap{} points below the undefended model on a corrupted one.
CK-PLUG goes furthest. Under BadRAG's tone attack, which tries to make the
model answer in a negative tone, the undefended model takes on that tone at
most \ECkSentUndefMax\% of the time. With CK-PLUG it takes it on
\ECkSentOne\% of the time with one poisoned passage in ten and
\ECkSentTop\% with five, so the defense increases the attack's effect
rather than reducing it. SCR, the one method in this family that asks the
model to judge between its own answer and the document's, gains
\EScrSatGain{} points on a corrupted context and costs \EScrCleanCost{} on a
clean one, roughly five times what it gains.

The other three methods are why this is a claim about a design and not about
all defenses. DyPRAG turns the document into model weights, so it has no
trust setting to turn. The two RobustRAG implementations answer from each
passage separately and take a vote, which asks which passages agree rather
than how far to trust passages in general. Figure~\ref{fig:dose} shows the
seven methods as the number of poisoned passages rises: CK-PLUG and DyPRAG
sit more than twenty points below the undefended model before anything is
planted and stay there, and the other four stay within a few points of it.

Suppressing the model's memory hurts only when the memory had the right
answer. Under BadRAG's refusal attack, which makes the
model refuse rather than answer wrongly, ParamMute protects strongly. With one
poisoned passage in ten the undefended model refuses \EPmDosUndefRef\% of the
time and answers \EPmDosUndefAcc\% correctly. ParamMute cuts refusals to
\EPmDosDefRef\% and restores accuracy to \EPmDosDefAcc\%, within
\EPmDosCleanGap{} points of its clean score. The mechanism is the same as
above. Suppressing memory removes a right answer when memory had one,
and removes the conflict behind the refusal when it did not.

\paragraph{Second direction: a security defense facing honest mistakes.}
\label{sec:transfer}
A defense built against an attacker helps against an
honest mistake only while the mistaken passages are few, and only when they
plant a false claim. We ran RobustRAG, in both its released implementations,
against four failures that no attacker wrote.

Against a real passage whose answer has changed, RobustRAG helps with one or
two such passages and then turns harmful. With one, the model gives the
outdated answer \EStaleUndefAsrOne\% of the time undefended and
\EStaleKeyAsrOne\% and \EStaleDecAsrOne\% with the two implementations. With
five, the defended model gives it more often than the undefended one,
\EStaleKeyAsrTop\% and \EStaleDecAsrTop\% against \EStaleUndefAsrTop\%, and
answers correctly less often, \EStaleKeyAccTop\% and \EStaleDecAccTop\%
against \EStaleUndefAccTop\%. A passage asserting a third, wrong answer shows
the same pattern: \ERobustDistKeyTwo\% and \ERobustDistDecTwo\% against
\ERobustDistUndefTwo\% undefended with two such passages, but
\ERobustDistKeyFive\% and \ERobustDistDecFive\% against
\ERobustDistUndefFive\% with five. Against a passage retrieved for the wrong
question, which plants nothing, it never helps: both implementations start
below the undefended model, \ERobustMisKeyClean\% and \ERobustMisDecClean\%
against \ERobustMisUndefClean\%, and are still below it with five such
passages. Against unrelated passages that lengthen the context,
there is nothing to act on. Five of them lower the undefended model's
accuracy by \NDilUndefDrop{} points, from \EDilUndefAccClean\% to
\EDilUndefAccFive\%, and the defended model differs from the undefended one
by at most \NDilGapMax{} points at any number of added passages, inside the
\EDilMdr{}-point threshold. That difference is the cost RobustRAG shows with
nothing added.

Against attacks from its own literature, RobustRAG stops PoisonedRAG, as its
own paper reports, by \ERobustPoisonedGain{} points. Against corpus poisoning
it neither helps nor hurts: \ERobustDispOneDef\% against
\ERobustDispOneUndef\% with one displaced passage and \ERobustDispFiveDef\%
against \ERobustDispFiveUndef\% with five, both inside the
\ERobustDispMdr{}-point threshold.

\paragraph{Every defense against every attack.}
\label{sec:grid}
Across every defense and every attack on open Natural
Questions, almost nothing helps. Table~\ref{tab:grid} reads each pair against
its own undefended model at five poisoned passages in ten. Of the
\NGridCells{} pairs, \NGridGainTop{} help by more than their threshold,
\NGridLoseTop{} hurt by more than their threshold, and \NGridFlatTop{} show
no measurable difference. All \NGridGainTop{} gains are against the refusal
attack, where the method helps by getting the model to answer again rather
than by resisting anything. Every measurable result against an attack that
plants a false answer is a loss. CK-PLUG and DyPRAG account for
\NGridLoseTopTwo{} of the \NGridLoseTop{} losses, and they are also the only
two methods that cost accuracy with nothing planted, more than twenty points
each. The other \NGridFreeClean{} of \NGridCells{} pairs cost nothing
measurable on a clean context.

\label{sec:lowdose}
Even with nine of ten passages honest, almost nothing
helps. One poisoned passage in ten is the default of the released attack and
the setting most favorable to a defense, because it leaves the most honest
evidence to work with. \NGridLowHelps{} of the \NGridLowPairs{} pairs help
there, and both are again the refusal attack. We report the worst result
for each defense rather than the average, because an average across attacks
would hide a complete failure against one of them.

The agent experiment pairs only security methods with security attacks, so
it does not test transfer, but it shows where a defense has to act to hold.
\NAgentHolds{} of \NAgentConditions{} defense and attack combinations drive
the attack's success to zero, and every one of them acts before the agent
reads the injected text or before it acts on it. None of the
\NAgentTriedReasoning{} combinations that act while the model reasons holds.
In this experiment, the stage at which a defense acts separates the defenses
that hold from those that do not.

\paragraph{Why neither design covers both.}
\label{sec:neither}
The two directions fail for opposite reasons. Each design rests on an
assumption that holds in its own regime and that the other regime breaks.
The safety fixes assume the document is honest: they decide how far to trust
it over the model's memory, and in the first direction an attacker writes the
document. RobustRAG assumes that wrong passages are few: its vote is sound
while honest passages outnumber corrupted ones, which holds when an attacker
can plant only a few passages, the setting RobustRAG certifies. An honest
mistake has no such limit. A fact that changes can leave many retrieved
passages wrong in the same way at once, and with five such passages in ten
the vote returns
the outdated answer more often than the undefended model does. A fix that
covers both regimes can therefore rely on neither assumption. It cannot trust
the document by default, and it cannot count on wrong passages being a
minority.

Answer to RQ2. No, in neither direction. Methods built for honest error lose
to an attacker when they shift the model's trust toward the document. A
method built against an attacker helps with an honest mistake only while the
mistaken passages are few, does more harm than no defense once they are
many, and has nothing to act on when the mistake plants no claim.

\paragraph{The form of the planted passage.}
\label{sec:payloads}
What a defense does depends on the form of the planted
passage, not on which attack it came from. ACD is the only method that both
uses a clean document and resists corpus poisoning: it ends
\EAcdSatGain{} points above the undefended model on a corrupted context.
Corpus poisoning, though, was optimized to fool a retriever, not ACD, so this
shows only that ACD resists that one passage. We then planted a fluent false
sentence instead, holding everything else fixed. Table~\ref{tab:acd} shows
the protection disappear. ACD keys on how uncertain a passage makes the
model. An optimized token string is incoherent and raises uncertainty, so ACD
discounts it. A fluent false sentence lowers uncertainty, so ACD trusts it
more, and the defended model ends below a model given no document at all.

Three more methods show the same dependence on form
(Table~\ref{tab:payloads}). ParamMute is harmful against both forms, by
\EParamExcessCorpus{} and \EParamExcessFalse{} points, but how well it
suppresses the model's wrong memory falls from \EParamCorpusPmDrop{} to
\EParamFalsePmDrop{} points. Under corpus poisoning the wrong answer lives
only in memory, so suppressing memory removes it. Under false evidence it
lives in memory and in the planted passage, and ParamMute reaches only the
first. CK-PLUG's effect on accuracy moves from \ECkCleanCost{} to
\ECkSatDelta{} points under corpus poisoning and stays near
\ECkFalseOffset{} under false evidence.
COIECD changes nothing measurable against either.

We do not rank the two forms. Before running them we wrote down the result
that would contradict our explanation: a defense doing better against false
evidence than against corpus poisoning. CK-PLUG does, with \ECkExcessFalse{}
points of excess damage against \ECkExcessCorpus{}. Both forms leave the
undefended model near the floor, at \ECkUndefFalseCorrupt\% and
\ECkUndefCorpusCorrupt\% for CK-PLUG and \ECoiecdFalseCorrupt\% and
\ECoiecdCorpusCorrupt\% for COIECD, so excess damage compares two near-total
collapses and has little room to separate them. We report CK-PLUG's result as
an exception this data cannot resolve, and compare defenses only within one
form.

\paragraph{Where the answers go.}
\label{sec:distractor}
No defense moves a measurable share of answers toward
the real document.
When the planted passage repeats what the model already believes, a correct
answer from memory and a copied answer look the same. We therefore built a
passage asserting a third answer, neither the true one nor the model's own,
by changing one word in the item's own evidence sentence. An output can then
be read three ways: the true answer means the model read the real document,
its own answer means it recalled from memory, and the third answer means it
copied the planted passage. With nothing planted the third answer appears
\EDistCleanBase\% of the time, and \EDistCleanNoRet\% of the time from a
model given no document.

ParamMute suppresses answers from memory, and the suppressed answers go to
the attacker. It cuts answers from memory from \EPmDistPmBase\% to \EPmDistPmDef\%, and copying rises from
\EPmDistCopyBase\% to \EPmDistCopyDef\% almost one for one, while correct
answers stay below the threshold. ACD does the reverse. It cuts copying from
\EAcdDistCopyBase\% to \EAcdDistCopyDef\%, and the answers it refuses to copy
go back to memory, not to the real document. CK-PLUG copies \ECkDistCopyDefCorrupt\% against its undefended
model's \ECkDistCopyBaseCorrupt\% (Table~\ref{tab:distractor}).

The two directions are one mechanism. Every one of these methods chooses
between the document and the model's memory, and the attack is inside the
document. Turning the setting toward the document gives answers to the
attacker; turning it toward memory gives them back to memory. Neither setting
reaches the real document, because the choice they make cannot separate a real
document from a planted one. Methods that turn toward memory also lose a share
of answers to neither outcome: \EAcdResid{}, \EScrResid{} and
\ESpareResid{} points for ACD, SCR and SpARE.

Answer to RQ3. A shared design choice. The methods fail in the same way
because they make the same choice, between the document and memory, and that
choice cannot separate a real document from a planted one.

\subsection{RQ4: Do the measurements the field leaves out change the result?}

\label{sec:controls}
Without a no-document score, a defense that ignores the
document looks immune to the attack. DyPRAG holds \EDypragClean\% on a clean
context and \EDypragSat\% on a corrupted one while the undefended model falls
from \EDypragRagClean\% to \EDypragRagSat\%. Read alone, that looks like
complete resistance. The same model with no document at all scores
\EDypragNoRet\%, which is not measurably different. DyPRAG is unaffected
because it is not reading the document, and only a no-document score shows
this.

Where two implementations of one design agree, the
result belongs to the design. RobustRAG ships two implementations that share
the idea of answering from each passage and voting, and share no code after
that. Under BadRAG's tone attack both stay at or below \NRobustToneMax\%,
across both implementations and two base models, where CK-PLUG on the same
attack reaches \ECkSentTop\%. The tone attack succeeds against methods that
trust the document, not against methods that vote across passages. Under the refusal attack neither
implementation changes the result measurably on either base model, within the
range the attack authors evaluate.

Answer to RQ4. One omitted measurement reverses a result: a no-document score
turns the campaign's apparently strongest defense into one that is not reading
its input. Two further checks, a second implementation and a second base
model, leave the results inside the evaluated range unchanged.

\subsection{RQ5: Does that explanation survive an adaptive attacker?}

\label{sec:adaptive}
The one method that resisted loses more than half of
its protection to an attacker who reads its rule. Every attack above was
built without knowledge of the defense it met. Such an attack bounds attack
strength from below: it is conservative evidence when a defense fails and
weak evidence when a defense holds. ACD was our only holding result, so we
attacked it directly.

ACD blends the model's answer with and without the document, weighted by how
uncertain each one is. The attacker controls only the document, so the attack
has one goal: make the document look certain. This also sets a limit. A
perfect attack of this kind turns ACD into the undefended model and cannot
push it below that. We committed to that limit, and five further predictions,
before building the attack.

We built two attacks. The first tries five phrasings of the same false claim
for each item and keeps the one ACD finds most certain. The second appends a
short suffix found by gradient search against the same goal. Both keep the
false answer fixed, so any change comes from the form alone.

All six predictions held (Table~\ref{tab:adaptive}). The fair measure is
ACD's margin over the undefended model, because both attacks also hurt the
undefended model. The rephrasing attack removes \PctAcdRemovedBestOf\% of
that margin. The gradient attack removes \PctAcdRemovedGradient\%, while doing
less damage to the undefended model than the rephrasing attack, so it is
attacking ACD's rule specifically. With nothing planted ACD and the
undefended model differ by at most \NAcdGapCleanMax{} points under all three
passages, so the attacks act only through the planted text. ACD
degrades but does not collapse: by the bands we set in advance it keeps some
protection, and it never falls below the undefended model.

Answer to RQ5. The explanation survives, and the one exception shrinks. The
method that resisted did so against attacks not built for it, and loses most
of that resistance to one that is. In our coding of their papers, the methods
in this family were validated only against incidental failure, so their
published results contain no test of this kind.
