\section{Results: controls and defenses that hold}
\label{sec:results2}

Section~\ref{sec:results} reports a negative result about one family of
defenses. This section answers RQ4 by reporting three things that
complicate it: three controls without
which a result reads the other way, a test of whether a result belongs to a
principle or to one codebase, and the defenses that did transfer.

One reading would make that negative result uninteresting: that the
communities never tested across the boundary because they already knew the
answer, so an experiment confirming it adds nothing. Three measurements say
otherwise. The communities do not read each other, and a judgement cannot be
informed by work nobody cites: \PctSecCitesSafety{}\% of security-regime
papers cite a safety-regime paper, and
\NOutcomeBothSides{} of \NOutcomePairs{} reported pairs carry an account
from both sides. \NDefClaimExceedsTest{} defenses claim an adversarial
threat model in their own evaluation coding while putting no adversary in
the experiment, which is not what withholding a test you expect to fail
looks like. And the result is not that the methods do nothing. They help on
clean context and then do harm under attack: the sign flips, which no
expectation of ``it will not transfer'' predicts. One method did resist, and
it took an adaptive attacker to price it.

\subsection{Controls that reverse a reading}

Each of the following reverses if the arm beside it is dropped, and in each
case that arm is one the relevant literature does not report.

\textbf{A no-retrieval baseline.} DyPRAG is perfectly
flat under an attack that destroys the undefended arm, scoring
\EDypragClean\% on a clean context and \EDypragSat\% on a corrupted one, across
the dose range while the undefended arm falls from \EDypragRagClean\% to
\EDypragRagSat\%. Read alone, that is the strongest defense result in the
campaign. The closed-book arm scores \EDypragNoRet\% and is statistically
indistinguishable from it.
DyPRAG is flat because it is not reading the document, not because it
is resisting the attack. Only a no-retrieval arm separates immunity from
inattention, and no paper in this family reports one.

\textbf{An abstention axis.} COIECD is inert on accuracy at every dose. Dumping
and classifying every generation shows it is not inert at all. On the items
where the undefended model answered from memory, COIECD converts
32.1\% of those answers into explicit abstentions, and does so only
under attack; on clean context the same pairing moves almost nothing. Because
the parametric answer is wrong by construction in this data, what it suppressed
were confident wrong answers and what it produced instead was "not mentioned".
That is a real safety gain, and it is invisible in the accuracy column that
every paper in this family reports.

\textbf{A model control.} RobustRAG ran on Mistral and the
context-reliance family ran on Llama, because those are the models each
paper released. Re-running RobustRAG on the other family's model changes
outcomes. The tone-steering finding survives unchanged, which strengthens
it. The implementation divergence below does not. At nine poisoned passages
against refusal induction, KeywordAgg is \NKeywordNineMistral{}\pp{} on
Mistral and \NKeywordNineLlama{}\pp{} on Llama: the variant that is
resolvably protective on one model is not protective on the other, while
DecodingAgg stays harmful on both (\NDecodingNineMistral{}\pp{} and
\NDecodingNineLlama{}\pp{}). Holding defense and attack fixed and changing
only the base model changes an effect's size, its existence, and in one case
its sign.

\subsection{Principle or implementation}

A single implementation cannot tell you whether you have found a property of a
design principle or a quirk of one codebase. RobustRAG
ships two aggregation variants that share the isolate-then-aggregate principle
and share no decision logic downstream of it. One extracts keywords and
re-prompts; the other averages next-token distributions.

Both show the same behavior under tone steering, at or below 0.3\% across
four cells and two models, against CK-PLUG's 99.0\% on the same attack and
model. Aggregation does not open the tone-steering hole; context-reliance
does. Two implementations that share only the principle agree here, so the
behavior belongs to the principle.

The caution runs the other way too. Across fifteen dose levels the two
implementations agree within noise on thirteen. On one they do not. At nine
poisoned passages in ten, against refusal induction, KeywordAgg is
\NKeywordNineMistral{}\pp{} and DecodingAgg is
\NDecodingNineMistral{}\pp{}, with both ends clearing the \ERobustMdr\pp
threshold at $n=300$. One implementation of isolate-then-aggregate is
resolvably protective and the other is resolvably harmful, on the same
attack, at the same dose, inside one codebase --- on this model. The control
above shows the protective end does not survive a change of base model, so
the divergence is a property of the pair, not of the principle.

The gap grows as the honest minority of passages shrinks: 13.0 points at five
in ten, 29.7 at nine in ten. It then collapses to 1.7 at ten in ten, where no
honest passage remains and the two implementations have nothing left to
disagree about. A survey reporting one number per published method would have
missed all of this.

\subsection{The grid on the other item set}
\label{sec:grid}

Everything above runs on CoConflictQA, where an item carries one context
passage and a dose is a fraction of the chunks that passage is cut into.
The campaign also runs every defense against every attack on open Natural
Questions, where a dose is $j$ of the top ten retrieved passages.
Table~\ref{tab:grid} is that grid: \NGridCells{} cells, each read against
its own undefended arm on the same items at the same dose. The two item sets
are not comparable and we do not compare them. What the grid adds is breadth:
the design rule of Section~\ref{sec:results} was read off one attack, and
here it meets four.

Across that breadth almost nothing helps. Of the \NGridCells{} cells,
\NGridGainTop{} show the defended arm ahead at the top dose by more than
that run's detectability threshold, \NGridLoseTop{} show it behind by more
than the threshold, and \NGridFlatTop{} resolve to no difference. All
\NGridGainTop{} gains are against the denial-of-service payload, where the
undefended model refuses to answer and any method that restores an answer
gains accuracy for a reason that is not robustness. Every cell that resolves
against a payload which plants a false answer rather than suppressing one
resolves downward.

The losses are concentrated the way Section~\ref{sec:results} predicts.
CK-PLUG and DyPRAG hold \NGridLoseTopTwo{} of the \NGridLoseTop{}: both
suppress the model's own parameters to force reliance on retrieved text, and
both pay for it on every payload they meet.

The clean column prices them. \NGridFreeClean{} of the \NGridCells{} cells
cost nothing measurable when no passage is planted, so most of these methods
are free to leave switched on. The \NGridCostClean{} that are not belong to
the same two methods, and both pay more than twenty points before any
passage is planted.

Two mechanisms in this grid are not yet complete. A stale document and a
diluted window are running, and we report no numbers for them here.

\subsection{Defenses that transfer}
\label{sec:transfer}

The exceptions are informative. RobustRAG stops PoisonedRAG, which its own
paper tested, and we reproduce that (+\ERobustPoisonedGain{}\pp). Against
corpus poisoning it neither helps nor hurts anywhere in the range we report:
\ERobustDispOneDef{}\% against the undefended arm's \ERobustDispOneUndef{}\%
at one displaced passage and \ERobustDispFiveDef{}\% against
\ERobustDispFiveUndef{}\% at five, both gaps inside the
\ERobustDispMdr{}\pp{} threshold. Refilling the window from ranks the
attacker does not hold moves it to \ERobustBackfillFive{}\% at five, also
inside the threshold.

One condition past that range says why, and we report it as a diagnostic
rather than as a result. When the attacker holds every retrieved passage both
arms sit on the floor, \ERobustDispTenDef{}\% and
\ERobustDispTenUndef{}\%, and the same refill restores
\ERobustBackfillTen{}\%. What decides that cell is not the defense but the
attacker's budget against the retrieval depth. Isolate-then-aggregate needs
an honest passage to aggregate, and whether one exists is a property of the
pipeline rather than of the method. The defense's own authors sweep a
corruption size up to half the window and no further, so we draw no verdict
about the defense from a condition they do not evaluate.

The same defense meets the other regime twice, and the two cells disagree.
Against distractor evidence --- a passage asserting a wrong answer, which is
what RobustRAG's threat model describes with the attacker taken out --- it
holds at low dose. At two planted passages in ten the keyword variant scores
\ERobustDistKeyTwo{}\% and the decoding variant \ERobustDistDecTwo{}\%,
against the undefended arm's \ERobustDistUndefTwo{}\%. The hold does not
survive dosing: at ten in ten both sit near \ERobustDistKeyTen{}\% against
\ERobustDistUndefTen{}\%. Against misretrieval it never helps. Both defended
arms start below the undefended one, \ERobustMisKeyClean{}\% and
\ERobustMisDecClean{}\% against \ERobustMisUndefClean{}\%, and both are
still below it at five in ten, \ERobustMisKeyFive{}\% and
\ERobustMisDecFive{}\% against \ERobustMisUndefFive{}\%. Misretrieval
plants nothing, so isolate-then-aggregate has no dishonest passage to isolate
and the defense pays its accuracy cost for nothing.

A defense built for an adversary transfers on the shape of the stimulus, not
on the presence of one. Where an incidental mechanism puts a wrong claim in
the window it is worth running. Where it only degrades what the retriever
returns it is a cost.

Across the agent cell, \NAgentHolds{} of \NAgentConditions{} conditions show
the technique holding, with attack success driven to zero. A further 4 show a
resolvable reduction. The rest divide between 19 with no resolvable effect at
that sample size and 9 with no reduction at all.

Every hold is at ingestion or at execution. Of the
\NAgentHolds{} that hold, \NAgentHoldsIngestion{} act at ingestion and
\NAgentHoldsExecution{} at execution, which is every execution-stage
technique we ran. None of the \NAgentTriedReasoning{}
reasoning-stage conditions holds. The stage a technique acts at, rather than
the technique, predicts whether it survives here --- the division
Section~\ref{sec:parallel} finds in the literature, reproduced in what the
methods actually do.

\subsection{The dose of maximum advantage}
\label{sec:lowdose}

A result read at the top of the dose range is read where the attack is
strongest and the measurement is weakest. The fewer honest passages remain,
the more a low accuracy measures the attack rather than a failing defense.

So we report the other end as well, and the attacks supply it. One poisoned
passage in ten is the default in the released attack constructor, and it is
the dose most favorable to a defense: nine honest passages remain for it to
work with. If a method can discount a planted passage while still using a
good one, this is where it shows.

\NGridLowHelps{} of the \NGridLowPairs{} pairs in Table~\ref{tab:grid} show
a resolvable benefit there. Every other defense is at or below its own
undefended arm, at the dose that favors it most, against an attack run at
its authors' own strength. Both exceptions are the denial-of-service payload,
where the undefended model refuses to answer and any method that restores an
answer gains accuracy for a reason that is not robustness.

The table is also the worst-case reading that a security claim requires. A
defense beaten by one of three payloads is not two-thirds robust, it is broken,
and averaging across payloads would hide that. We therefore report the
minimum rather than the mean: robustness is a property of the strongest
attack a method has met, not of the attacks its authors chose.

\subsection{Adaptive attackers}
\label{sec:adaptive}

This subsection answers RQ5. Every result above comes from an attack that was not built for the defense it
met. Section~\ref{sec:experiments} states why that is conservative for a
failure and invalid for a resistance. ACD is our one resistance result, so it
is the one that has to be tested properly.

ACD mixes the no-context and with-context next-token distributions by
\[
d_\alpha = \frac{H}{H_{\mathrm{ctx}} + H},
\qquad
s \leftarrow s + d_\alpha\,(s_{\mathrm{ctx}} - s),
\]
where $H$ is the Shannon entropy of the no-context distribution and
$H_{\mathrm{ctx}}$ that of the with-context one. $H$ is a property of the
question. $H_{\mathrm{ctx}}$ is conditioned on the retrieved context, which an
attacker who owns the corpus controls outright. So the attack has
exactly one objective: minimize $H_{\mathrm{ctx}}$.

The ceiling follows from the rule, not from an experiment. As
$H_{\mathrm{ctx}} \to 0$, $d_\alpha \to 1$ and the update collapses to
$s_{\mathrm{ctx}}$ unchanged, which is the undefended model. A perfect adaptive
attack of this kind turns ACD into plain RAG and can do no better. It cannot
drive ACD \emph{below} its own undefended arm. $d_\alpha$ is bounded in
$(0,1)$, and the endpoints are closed-book and plain RAG. We committed that
bound, and five further predictions, before building the payload. An ACD arm
landing below the undefended arm would have falsified our account of the
mechanism.

The attack is white-box and query-based. Five candidate passages per item, all
asserting the \emph{same} distractor answer and differing only in form, each
scored by the $H_{\mathrm{ctx}}$ ACD itself would compute, keeping the
minimum.

The attacker optimizes on the items it then attacks. This is not training on
the test set. The threat model gives the adversary the model and the defense rule, and an
adversary who owns a corpus tunes the passage they are about to plant. A payload
optimized on held-out items and transferred would measure something else, and
something weaker. What is held out is the few-shot block: its demonstrations
are drawn from items outside the evaluation slice and are never poisoned, so
the defense is not shown the attack in its own prompt. Holding the asserted answer fixed is what makes any movement
attributable to form rather than to a different claim. It is a deliberately
weak optimizer, so what it achieves is again a lower bound.

All six predictions held, and Table~\ref{tab:adaptive} carries
them. $H_{\mathrm{ctx}}$ falls 42\%, $d_\alpha$ rises toward 1, ACD's copy rate rises from \EAcdDistCopyDef\% to
\EAcdAdaptCopy\% ($p = 9.5\times10^{-18}$), its parametric recall falls
toward the undefended arm's, and its accuracy lands on \EAcdAdaptAcc\% ---
exactly the undefended arm, and below the no-retrieval floor of
\EAcdAdaptNoRet\%. ACD does not fall below the
undefended arm on copy rate, as the ceiling requires.

The honest size of the effect is the gap, not ACD's rise. The adaptive
payload is stronger against the undefended arm as well, so reporting only
ACD's change would credit the defense with damage the attack does to everyone.
Against its own undefended arm the protection falls from \NAcdGapBase{}\pp{}
to \NAcdGapBestOf{}\pp{}: \PctAcdRemovedBestOf{}\% of it removed by a
best-of-five search over sentence templates. Not all of it. $d_\alpha$ reached 0.653 of a possible 1.0, which
left a question the weak optimizer could not answer.

A gradient search answers it. We ran a GCG-style~\cite{zou-2023-universal} coordinate search
over a 20-token suffix against the same objective, appended to the passage
best-of-five had already chosen so that the asserted answer stays fixed and
any movement is the gradient's. Candidate tokens are restricted to
space-leading printable ASCII, because a suffix that does not survive a
decode and re-encode is not a passage an attacker can place in a corpus; the
round trip is exact on all \NAcdAdaptiveItems{} items. A gradient passage is kept only where it
beats the best-of-five passage on the reported measure, which it fails to do
on 163 of \NAcdAdaptiveItems{}, so the payload is monotone by construction.

It drives $H_{\mathrm{ctx}}$ to 0.614 and $d_\alpha$ to 0.708, and the
defensive gap falls to \NAcdGapGradient{}\pp{}: \PctAcdRemovedGradient{}\% of ACD's protection removed,
twice what sentence templates took. The \NAcdGradientBeyond{}\pp{} it takes beyond
best-of-five clears the \NAcdAdaptiveMdr{}\pp{} detectability threshold at this
sample size.

The gradient payload is weaker in general and stronger against the
defense. It damages the undefended arm \emph{less} than best-of-five
does, \EAcdGradUndefCopy\% against \EAcdAdaptUndefCopy\%, while taking
considerably more from ACD. The gap
is what isolates the defense, and the gap is what moved. The search therefore attacks the method rather than simply producing a
better payload.

The two conditions agree. On a clean context the three payloads are
indistinguishable: no gap between the arms exceeds \NAcdGapCleanMax{}\pp{},
because there is nothing there to defend against. They separate only once
the context is corrupted, which is what one expects of a rule keyed on a
statistic computed over the whole context.

ACD degrades under adaptive pressure; it does not collapse. We
committed the bands in advance: below 10\pp{} of remaining gap we would have
called it neutralized, and 10 to 25\pp{} degraded further while holding
something back. The result is the second. A rule that gives up
\PctAcdRemovedGradient{}\% of its
protection to an attacker with gradients is a real obstacle that has now been
priced, rather than either a solved problem or a broken one.

What this licenses is still narrow. It does not show ACD is broken, and the
ceiling in the rule says no attack of this kind could have driven it below the
undefended arm. One defense in our campaign looked like a counterexample. It
gives up most of its protection to an attacker who reads the method. Every
published evaluation of a context-reliance defense in our corpus is
non-adaptive. The gap between non-adaptive and adaptive
evaluation is large enough to change the verdict.
