LLM agents answer from their context, so an attacker who controls the context
controls the answer. Two research communities study this failure, and neither
tests the other's work. Security researchers study context an attacker changes
on purpose; safety researchers study context that becomes wrong on its own. The
model cannot tell the difference.

We read \NCorpus{} papers in full and recovered every confirmed (defense,
attack) pair either community reports. Only a handful cross between them, and
\textbf{exactly \NSecDefVsSafetyAttack{} security defense has ever been
evaluated against a safety failure mechanism}. The two sides do not duplicate
each other. They divide the problem instead. Security studies how the bad text
arrives and defends where it enters. Safety studies why the model believes it
and defends where the model reasons. A defense at the reasoning stage judges
text the attacker has already chosen.

We then ran the experiments neither community has run: \NCampaignPairs{}
pairs, each from its authors' released code and weights, against an
undefended model on the same items. \textbf{Methods built for honest error
decide how far to trust the document, and that choice hands the answer to an
attacker who wrote it.} Within the range of poisoned passages the attack
authors themselves evaluate, \NGridGainTop{} of \NGridCells{} defense and
attack pairs help, and all \NGridGainTop{} help only by getting the model to
answer again after an attack made it refuse. The one defense that resisted
loses \PctAcdRemovedGradient{}\% of its protection to an attacker who reads
its rule. We report results for models of 7 to 14 billion parameters, with
one dataset per experiment.
