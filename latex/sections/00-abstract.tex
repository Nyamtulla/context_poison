Two research communities study the same failure in large language model
agents, and neither tests the other's work. Security researchers study context
an attacker changes on purpose, such as indirect prompt injection, retrieval
poisoning and memory backdoors, and they publish attacks and defenses. Machine
learning researchers study context that becomes wrong on its own, such as
retrieval noise, knowledge conflict and long-context degradation, and they
publish mechanisms and fixes. The vocabularies differ; the failure does not.
In both cases the model cannot tell which part of its context deserves trust.

We measure the separation rather than assert it. From \NCorpus{} papers read in
full we recover \NPureTrackPairs{} confirmed (defense, attack) pairs in which
some paper evaluated some defense against some attack, and we date every one.
Only \NCrossCommunityPairs{} of them (\PctCrossCommunity{}\%) cross between the
two communities, and one direction is empty outright: \textbf{no security
defense in our corpus has ever been evaluated against a machine learning
failure mechanism}. Not once in \NPureTrackPairs{} pairs. The experiment the
field most needs is the one it has never run.

\textbf{So we ran it.} We built a uniform platform and executed
\NCampaignPairs{} (defense, attack) pairs across \NCampaignImpl{}
implementations and \NCampaignEpisodes{} scored episodes, each from its
authors' released code and weights, under an attack-works-first control and a
fixed detectability threshold. \NOursCrossPairs{} of our \NOursPairs{}
retrieval-cell pairs cross the community boundary, against
\PctCrossCommunity{}\% of the literature's.

\textbf{The contribution is not that defenses fail. It is why, and it is one
mechanism.} ParamMute suppresses the model's parametric memory and the
attacker fills the vacancy, raising the rate at which the model copies planted
text from 43.9\% to 81.4\%. ACD does the opposite, suppressing the suspicious
document, and memory fills the vacancy instead. Both defenses succeed at their
stated goal; neither improves accuracy. \textbf{Neither moves probability mass
toward the real document, because neither can tell the real document from the
planted one.} Both arbitrate between \emph{context} and \emph{parameters}, and
the attack lives inside the context. To show this is relocation rather than
loss we built a payload that plants a third answer, neither the truth nor the
model's prior, which separates copying from remembering: across three
context-reliance defenses the memory lost and the copying gained cancel to
within 7 points. These methods move correctness; they do not create it.

The finding survives an adaptive attacker. ACD's decision rule is public, so we
optimized a payload against the single quantity it measures. That removes 29\%
of its protection and drives its accuracy onto the undefended arm and below a
no-retrieval baseline.

Finally we report three controls the literature omits, each of which overturned
a conclusion we would otherwise have published: a no-retrieval baseline, an
abstention axis, and a model control. We argue they belong in the reporting
conventions of both communities.
