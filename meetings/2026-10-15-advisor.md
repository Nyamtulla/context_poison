## 1. The decision

**The project stops being a SoK and becomes a research paper.**

The reasoning given: we have more contributions than a systematization can
carry, and the contributions we have are novel enough to stand on their own.
A survey frames our experiments as "tests the field skipped." A research paper
frames the same work as a platform plus a first-of-its-kind transfer study.

The taxonomy and the census do not get thrown away. They become the section
that earns the right to make the central claim, rather than being the point of
the paper.

## 2. The central contribution, and the correction it needs

### What was proposed

Study the **timeline** of attacks and defenses. If a defense published by
community A already defends an attack published later by community B, then
anyone now proposing a defense for that attack is reinventing a wheel that
exists, and does not know it because the two communities do not read each
other.

### What the data says

**[verified]** `scripts/build_timeline.py` now dates **all 349** confirmed
literature pairs. (The first pass resolved only 291; the other 58 were name
mismatches, chiefly the 14 attacks that exist only inside a benchmark harness
and so carry the benchmark paper's id. None are dropped.)

| | count | share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Defense predates the attack it was tested on | 50 | 14.3 % |
| Defense and attack from **different communities** | **4** | **1.2 %** |
| Both at once, the "reinventing the wheel" cell | **1** | **0.3 %** |

Rates are over the 346 pairs where both sides sit in one of the two pure
tracks; a paper coded "Both" cannot cross a boundary it sits on. Counting
those as crossing gives 7 of 349, 2.0 %. The single defense-first
cross-community pair is **Self-RAG (ML/AI, 2023) vs PoisonedRAG (Security,
2024)**.

### The cell nobody has ever filled

**[verified]** Breaking the 346 pairs out by which community each side came
from gives a cleaner statement than any percentage:

| defense from \ attack from | Security | ML/AI |
|---|---:|---:|
| **Security** | 262 | **0** |
| **ML/AI** | 4 | 80 |

**No security defense has ever been evaluated against an ML/AI failure
mechanism. Not once in 346 pairs.** 342 of 349 pairs (98.0 %) stay inside one
community.

### Where our campaign sits

**[verified]** The same matrix for the 28 pairs we executed ourselves:

| | literature | our campaign |
|---|---:|---:|
| cross-community pairs | 4 of 346 (1.2 %) | **16 of 28 (57 %)** |

We have **five times** the world's supply of ML/AI-defense-against-
Security-attack evidence: 16 new pairs against the 4 that existed. That is the
contribution, stated as a number.

### Why this is better than what was asked for

The question cannot be answered from the literature, because **the experiment
does not exist in the literature**. 1.4 % cross-community testing is consistent
with the citation rates we already measured, 11.7 % and 9.4 %. The communities
do not read each other, so they do not test each other.

That converts the contribution from a literature observation into an
experimental one, in three steps:

1. **Measurement.** The cross-community pair is tested in 1 of 291 cases.
2. **Execution.** We ran them. **8 of our 9 defense implementations are
   ML/AI-track methods run against Security-track attacks.** Our campaign is
   precisely the missing cell.
3. **Mechanism.** And we can say why they behave as they do.

### The correction to carry back

The proposed framing is "the defense already exists, so people are reinventing
the wheel." **Our evidence mostly says the opposite.** ML/AI defenses run
against Security attacks usually fail, often make matters worse, and sometimes
reverse sign depending on the model. Transfer does happen (ACD beat corpus
poisoning; CK-PLUG reduced PoisonedRAG on Mistral) but it is not predictable.

The defensible claim is stronger and more useful:

> **Transfer cannot be assumed in either direction, which is exactly why the
> pair has to be run.**

That is an argument for building the evaluation platform, which lands on the
DEEPSEC-style paper named in the meeting.

## 3. Manual validation becomes a statistical sample

### The critique, which is correct

Our 100-paper validation set was drawn as the **top 100 most load-bearing**
papers. That is a defensible engineering choice and it is **not a probability
sample**, so it supports no statistical claim about the other 882. A reviewer
would catch this.

### Problem to raise: grade A does not exist

**[verified]** The A–D scheme was defined in the project plan (A =
peer-reviewed with artifacts, B = peer-reviewed, C = credible preprint,
D = gray literature) but only two levels were ever assigned:

| grade | count |
|---|---:|
| A | **0** |
| B | 175 |
| C | 807 |
| D | **0** |

Equal sampling across three grades cannot run until this is fixed. We know
artifact availability for much of the corpus, so A is recoverable. **[to do]**

### The numbers

**[verified]** 95 % confidence, finite population correction, N = 982:

| margin of error | worst case (p = .50) | if agreement is ~85 % |
|---|---:|---:|
| **± 5 %** | **277** | 164 |
| ± 7 % | 164 | 91 |

**Recommendation: ± 5 %, worst case, n = 277**, run in two stages:

1. **The existing 100 papers become a pilot**, used to estimate the agreement
   rate *p*. They cannot count toward the statistical claim because they were
   purposively selected.
2. **Recompute n from the observed p**, then draw a fresh stratified random
   sample. If agreement is 85 %, n falls from 277 to 164.

### On allocation

Equal numbers per grade is right for *comparing* grades, but then the overall
rate must be **weighted** by stratum size (175/982 and 807/982) or grade B is
massively over-represented. Also, 139 papers from a stratum of 175 is a 79 %
census and wasteful.

If the goal is to show extraction is as good on preprints as on peer-reviewed
work, that is a two-proportion power calculation, not a margin of error:

| to detect | n per grade |
|---|---:|
| a 10 pp gap (.90 vs .80) | 199 |
| a 15 pp gap (.90 vs .75) | 100 |
| a 20 pp gap (.90 vs .70) | 62 |

**Open question for the next meeting:** which claim do we want? *"Our
extraction is X % accurate"* (n = 277) or *"extraction quality does not depend
on paper rigor"* (n = 100–199 **per grade**)? They are different studies.

## 4. Mechanism, not just direction

Across 84 executed pairs we mostly report "reduces / increases / not
resolvable." That tells nobody how to build a better defense.

The requirement: when a defense improves or degrades an attack, say **why**, so
the communities can build defenses that generalize.

We have one worked example already, the distractor-payload result: ParamMute
suppresses memory and the attacker fills the vacancy; ACD suppresses the
attacker and memory fills it; neither moves probability mass toward the real
document, because neither can tell the real document from the planted one.
Both only arbitrate between *context* and *parameters*, and the attack lives
inside the context.

**[to do]** Go back through the 84 pairs and ask "why" of each. Most will not
yield an answer. The ones that do become the generalization section.

## 5. Reference papers to study

Author lists corrected against the sources.

**[DEEPSEC: A Uniform Platform for Security Analysis of Deep Learning Model](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/8835375)**
Xiang Ling, Shouling Ji et al. Zhejiang University, IEEE S&P 2019.
16 attacks with 10 utility metrics, 13 defenses with 5 metrics, in one harness.
The structural template.

**[A critique of the DeepSec Platform](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.07112)**
Nicholas Carlini, 2019.
**Read this before copying the template.** He argues DEEPSEC's results are
"fundamentally flawed and misleading." A famous paper doing what we are about
to do was taken apart by the field's most careful adversarial-ML reviewer. His
specific objections tell us what our control-fires-first rule and detectability
thresholds have to survive. We already do some of this correctly, which is a
real advantage, but the design should be checked against his list line by line.

**["Get in Researchers; We're Measuring Reproducibility"](https://cise.ufl.edu/~butler/pubs/ccs23-olszewski.pdf)**
Olszewski, Lu, Stillman, Warren, Kitroser, Pascual, Ukirde, Traynor, Butler.
University of Florida, ACM CCS 2023, pp. 3433–3459.
The methodology template: how to sample papers, how to attempt reproduction,
how to report what failed and why. Our FaithfulRAG and CD2 write-ups are
already in this genre without having known it.

## 6. What we got wrong

Stated plainly, because the fixes follow from them.

- **We optimised for coverage instead of for a claim.** 982 papers, 221
  mechanisms, 527 defenses: all correct, all defensible, and it buries the one
  thing only we can say.
- **We never looked at time.** Every registry carries a year. We built
  channel × intent × consequence and never asked who came first. That was free
  and we skipped it.
- **We reported outcomes without mechanisms.** See §4.
- **Our validation sample was purposive, not random.** See §3.
- **We framed the experiments as "tests the field skipped,"** which is survey
  language for work that is actually a platform plus a transfer study.
