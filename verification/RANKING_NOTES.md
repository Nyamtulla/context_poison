# Paper importance ranking — what it is and how to use it

`verification/paper_importance_ranking.xlsx`, generated by
`scripts/rank_papers.py`. 984 papers, every component of the score stored
beside the total so a rank can be explained rather than trusted.

## The question it answers

**Which papers should a human verify first, because the project's claims rest
on them.** That is not the same as which papers are famous, and the score is
built to keep those apart.

## Score components

| component | weight | what it measures |
|---|---:|---|
| `internal` | **0.30** | in-degree in our own citation network — how many corpus papers cite it |
| `external` | 0.20 | log-scaled Semantic Scholar citations |
| `registry` | 0.15 | contributes a named attack, defense or incidental mechanism to RQ3/RQ4 |
| `tested` | 0.15 | appears in a confirmed RQ5 defence × mechanism pair |
| `ran` | 0.15 | **we executed this technique ourselves** |
| `engaged` | 0.05 | cites the corpus at all — a tie-break, not a driver |

`internal` carries the most weight on purpose. External citations measure
standing in the field; in-degree measures how much *this corpus* is built on
the paper, which is the thing that makes a coding error expensive here.

## Tiers — act on these, not on the score

| tier | n | why |
|---|---:|---|
| **A — we ran it** | **13** | an experiment in this campaign depends on this paper's coding |
| **A — top 50 by score** | 45 | highest combined importance |
| B | 144 | ranks 51–200 |
| C | 782 | the rest |

**The two A tiers are separate for a reason.** SHIFT and SABER rank #274 and
#252 on score — near-uncited, no in-degree — yet both carry a transfer result
in this campaign. If their channel or consequence coding is wrong, a published
experiment is mis-framed. Popularity and load-bearing are different questions
and the continuous score only answers the first, so the tier overrides it
rather than the weights being bent to hide the difference.

## Sanity checks it passes

- The top 100 is **98 % attack / defense / both** — only 2 papers coded
  `neither` reach it, which is what you want from a score meant to find the
  load-bearing work.
- All 13 papers we experimented on fall inside the top 274 on score alone,
  before the tier override.
- Top-100 consequence mix (goal-hijack 65, reasoning-corruption 19,
  silent-corruption 13) tracks the registry's own distribution rather than
  over-weighting the cell we happen to have worked in.

## What it is not

**This is not a probability sample.** Verifying the top of this list measures
whether the papers the project leans on are coded correctly — a real and
important question, and not the same as how good the extraction is overall.
The second question needs `verification/verification_sample.xlsx`, which is
drawn at random and carries sampling weights.

Keep the two results apart. An accuracy figure from this ranking describes
these papers and cannot be generalised, because importance is exactly the kind
of non-random selection that breaks the inference.
